## Governing inquiry
**WHAT HISTORY DOES THE MATERIAL CLAIM?**

THERE and HERE are relative positions in a material history, not moral rankings, an absolute organic/synthetic binary, or a claim that newer materials replace older ones universally. Each successor may become the predecessor of something else. Materials coexist. Do not assume all organic matter is good or all manufactured matter false.

## Material operations — possibilities, not steps
- **Substitution:** one material occupies another's office.
- **Inversion:** properties cross in the opposite direction.
- **Contamination:** one medium acquires another's behavior.
- **Succession:** one makes conditions for another.
- **Imitation:** appearance transfers without process or capacity.
- **Resistance:** a medium refuses to become its counterpart.
- **Misrecognition:** the receiver initially identifies the wrong material.
- **Inheritance:** a substitute carries an earlier history.
- **Displacement:** something ordinary occupies an impossible location.
- **Contradiction:** incompatible material histories coexist without resolution.
- **Erosion:** identity is worn away.
- **Return:** an earlier property appears in a successor.
- **Impossible provenance:** a mark claims an event its material could not have undergone.
- **Coexistence:** neither material absorbs the other.

Never make every graphic half-flesh/half-machine, a split-screen THEN/NOW, a wood-to-metal gradient, or a parade of materials. A single earned contradiction can outweigh many substitutions. Distinguish organic, manufactured, and digitally depicted histories without pretending an image literally possesses the depicted process.
