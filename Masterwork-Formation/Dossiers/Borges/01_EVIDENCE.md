# Borges — Evidence Notes

This file records source-supported evidence separately from project interpretation.

## Evidentiary survivability

Borges left a substantial but dispersed manuscript record.

Daniel Balderston's *How Borges Wrote* reconstructs his process from more than 170 manuscripts and primary documents and describes:
- notes from reading
- brainstorming
- notebooks
- multiple possible phrasings
- fair copies
- typescripts
- revisions
- fragments
- changes made after publication

The University of Virginia holds Borges manuscripts including "La biblioteca total," "El milagro secreto," "La casa de Asterión," "El muerto," and others.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Revision practice

Borges deliberately and repeatedly revised his work. Genetic scholarship shows manuscripts with deletions, insertions, alternatives, marginal symbols, and post-publication changes.

The Oxford Handbook notes that for some Borges texts the notion of one stable "final version" is difficult to sustain.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## "La biblioteca total" -> "La biblioteca de Babel"

The University of Virginia preserves the 1939 holograph manuscript "La biblioteca total," with numerous corrections and deletions.

The archive identifies it as an important precursor to "La biblioteca de Babel," published two years later.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## "El milagro secreto"

UVA preserves a holograph manuscript with many deletions, variants, and interpolations. Genetic scholarship has used it to examine Borges's insertion methods and compositional symbols.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Childhood and reading inheritance

Borges repeatedly described his father's library as formative. He read English early and encountered Stevenson, Wells, Poe, Twain, and other English-language writers young.

His family years in Europe during World War I exposed him to French education, German, Schopenhauer, Expressionism, and European avant-garde currents.

Status: DOCUMENTED / AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL EVIDENCE WITH NORMAL RETROSPECTIVE CAUTION.

## Early literary formation

Before the major fictions Borges had:
- written poetry
- participated in Ultraism
- written literary and philosophical essays
- translated
- reviewed books
- produced *Historia universal de la infamia*
- worked intensely with literary history, citation, commentary, and rewriting

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Municipal library period

Borges began work at the Miguel Cané Municipal Library in 1938.

The City of Buenos Aires records that while working there he read, translated, wrote notes and reviews, and produced stories later included in *Ficciones*, including "La biblioteca de Babel," "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," and "Pierre Menard."

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Father, accident, illness, and fiction turn

Borges's father died in 1938.

Borges then suffered a serious head injury and life-threatening infection.

In later interviews and autobiographical accounts, Borges connected his recovery with an experiment in writing a new kind of prose fiction, often naming "Pierre Menard" as a threshold.

Caution:
This retrospective account is not literally the beginning of all Borges fiction. Borges had written narrative prose before the accident.

## "Pierre Menard"

Published in *Sur* in 1939 and later gathered into *Ficciones*.

Borges later described it as a hybrid between story and essay and linked it to the idea that rereading changes a work.

Status: DOCUMENTED / AUTHORIAL RETROSPECTION.

## World War context

Modern scholarship emphasizes that World War I and World War II form important historical contexts for several canonical fictions.

Cambridge scholarship identifies explicit intertexts connected to war in "Pierre Menard" and "The Garden of Forking Paths."

Borges publicly opposed Nazism, fascism, antisemitism, and xenophobic nationalism during this period.

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS.

## Ficciones publication

*El jardín de senderos que se bifurcan* appeared in 1941.

The stories were incorporated into *Ficciones* in 1944.

The 1941 collection failed to receive Argentina's National Literary Prize, producing a literary controversy; *Sur* devoted attention to Borges's work.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Collaboration and false authors

Borges collaborated extensively with Adolfo Bioy Casares and, with Bioy, created pseudonymous and fictitious authors such as H. Bustos Domecq.

Modern scholarship treats invention of authors and apocryphal texts as central to Borges's challenge to literary authority and original authorship.

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

## Major evidence caution

Borges was an accomplished self-mythologizer in interviews and autobiographical statements.

Later accounts of origin should therefore be compared against:
- dated manuscripts
- periodical publication
- letters
- contemporaneous testimony
- genetic scholarship

Do not make the retrospective Borges the sole authority on how Borges became Borges.
