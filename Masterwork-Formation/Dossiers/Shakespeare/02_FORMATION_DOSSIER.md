# Shakespeare — Formation Dossier

## Unit of analysis

SHAKESPEAREAN DRAMATIC PRACTICE

with selected plays used as comparative evidence rather than treating all works as identical.

This dossier focuses on the authorship-spine problem:

HOW DOES A MAKER CREATE VOICES THAT HAVE LOCAL STANDING INSIDE A HIGHLY DESIGNED FORM?

---

## 1. Archive condition

The archive is paradoxical.

We possess:
- many early printed texts,
- company records,
- performance documents,
- source materials,
- theatrical evidence,
- later textual witnesses,
- evidence of collaboration.

But we possess almost no secure authorial draft material.

Therefore Shakespeare is not a Proust case.

We cannot routinely reconstruct:
FIRST DRAFT
-> REVISED DRAFT
-> FINAL AUTHORIAL COPY.

Instead, formation must often be inferred through:
SOURCE
-> SURVIVING PLAY
plus
THEATRICAL / PRINT / COMPANY EVIDENCE.

This makes source transformation especially important.

---

## 2. Inheritance

Shakespeare inherits a massive dramatic and textual field.

He works with:
- chronicles,
- classical history,
- romance,
- novella,
- revenge tragedy,
- morality traditions,
- Senecan tragedy,
- Plautine and Terentian comedy,
- native English drama,
- biblical language and rhetoric,
- contemporary political and legal discourse,
- actors and theatrical conventions,
- prior versions of stories already known to audiences.

The central formation question is therefore not:

WHERE DID SHAKESPEARE GET THE PLOT?

It is:

WHAT DOES SHAKESPEARE ADD, REMOVE, REDISTRIBUTE, OR EMBODY SO THAT AN INHERITED STORY BECOMES A DIFFERENT DRAMATIC PROBLEM?

This aligns with the earned principle that originality is transformation of inheritance.

---

## 3. Pressure

Shakespeare writes under pressures unlike those of the solitary modern novelist.

Relevant pressures include:
- need for performable plays,
- repertory demand,
- specific actors and company resources,
- venue architecture,
- patronage,
- censorship,
- audience diversity,
- commercial competition,
- actor memory and rehearsal,
- collaboration,
- eventual print publication that may not preserve one uncontested authorial state.

The important relation is:

THEATER PRESSURE
-> DRAMATIC ECONOMY
-> EMBODIED CONFLICT.

A dramatic idea must become something actors can do before an audience.

---

## 4. Problem trajectory

There is rarely a surviving private document saying:

"I am writing *Hamlet* to solve X."

So the operative problem must be reconstructed through source transformation and dramatic form.

Across selected plays, a recurring problem appears:

HOW CAN CONFLICTING HUMAN POSITIONS BE MADE TO ACT UPON ONE ANOTHER WITHOUT A NARRATOR CONTINUALLY EXPLAINING WHICH POSITION OWNS THE TRUTH?

This is a project inference.

Individual plays produce specific versions:

### Hamlet
An inherited revenge structure becomes a prolonged interrogative problem of:
- knowledge,
- evidence,
- interiority,
- performance,
- action.

### Othello
A tale of jealousy becomes a machine for:
- manipulated evidence,
- competing speech acts,
- trust,
- misrecognition,
- interpretation under pressure.

### King Lear
An inherited king-and-daughters story is combined with a second family plot and driven toward multiple simultaneous crises of:
- authority,
- kinship,
- recognition,
- speech,
- blindness.

### The Tempest
The playwright-like Prospero explicitly dramatizes condition-setting and the problem of relinquishing control.

The recurring migration is:

STORY
-> CHARACTER POSITION
-> EMBODIED CONFLICT
-> CONSEQUENCE.

---

## 5. Formation dynamics

Shakespearean formation is neither purely solitary nor simply collaborative.

It includes:

SOURCE TRANSFORMATION
+
INDIVIDUAL DRAMATIC COMPOSITION
+
COMPANY REALIZATION
+
ACTOR EMBODIMENT
+
PERFORMANCE
+
POSSIBLE CUTTING / REVISION
+
PRINT TRANSMISSION
+
POSTHUMOUS COLLECTION.

Some plays also include direct coauthorship.

Therefore the work has at least two lives:

PLAY-AS-WRITTEN / TRANSMITTED TEXT

and

PLAY-AS-PERFORMED EVENT.

The second cannot be reduced to the first.

This creates a new formation category:

EMBODIED REALIZATION.

---

## 6. Embodiment as authorship condition

Drama delegates speech to bodies.

A written character becomes:
- a voice,
- a face,
- movement,
- timing,
- gesture,
- silence,
- spatial relation,
- vulnerability before an audience.

This matters for "release of voice."

The author does not merely write:

IAGO BELIEVES X.

The author creates lines and situations an actor must inhabit.

Likewise:
- Hamlet's hesitation becomes duration,
- Lear's authority becomes vocal and bodily breakdown,
- Othello's trust becomes visible conversion,
- Desdemona's speech occupies the same stage as accusations against her,
- Emilia's final testimony becomes an embodied refusal.

Working principle:

EMBODIMENT GIVES LOCAL FORCE TO AUTHORED VOICE.

The voice remains authored.

But once embodied, it must operate as a position in the scene rather than as explanatory prose.

---

## 7. Actors as generative constraint

Shakespeare's relation to Richard Burbage is especially suggestive.

Burbage is associated with some of Shakespeare's most demanding central roles:
- Richard III
- Hamlet
- Othello
- Lear

Recent theatrical scholarship argues for a creative partnership in which Shakespeare could write with an actor's capacities in mind while also asking the actor to extend those capacities.

That relation is important because:

THE AUTHOR DOES NOT CREATE THE CHARACTER ALONE.

The role is completed through another person's body and skill.

This is not distributed textual authorship in the Homeric sense.

It is:

DISTRIBUTED REALIZATION.

That distinction should enter the instrument.

---

## 8. Decisive formal move

The strongest current formulation is:

DO NOT TELL THE AUDIENCE WHAT EVERYONE MEANS.

GIVE EACH POSITION A BODY, A VOICE, A MOTIVE, PARTIAL KNOWLEDGE, AND THE POWER TO ACT.

Then:

LET THE POSITIONS COLLIDE UNDER CONSEQUENCE.

This is more precise than "Shakespeare creates complex characters."

The dramatic field gains autonomy-like force because:
- speech is distributed,
- knowledge is uneven,
- motives conflict,
- action changes conditions,
- the author does not continuously adjudicate from outside.

---

## 9. Authorial absence inside dramatic presence

Shakespeare is everywhere in construction.

But unlike Dante's retrospective narrator or Proust's narrating consciousness, Shakespeare usually does not enter the play as a stable authorial voice that explains the whole.

There are:
- choruses,
- prologues,
- epilogues,
- fools,
- metatheatrical figures,
- soliloquies,
- asides.

But none consistently functions as:

THE AUTHOR'S FINAL INTERPRETIVE COURT.

This matters.

The absence of an authoritative narrator creates room for local voices to acquire standing.

Working relation:

MAXIMUM CONSTRUCTION
+
MINIMUM DIRECT AUTHORIAL ADJUDICATION.

This may be the deep operation behind "release of voice."

---

## 10. Soliloquy does not equal truth

The soliloquy gives privileged access to character speech.

But it does not guarantee final truth.

Richard III can declare motives while also performing himself.

Hamlet can reason brilliantly while remaining uncertain or self-divided.

Iago offers motives that may not fully stabilize his motivation.

Macbeth interprets his own desire while being changed by action.

Therefore Shakespeare's interiority is not simply:

CHARACTER TELLS US WHO THEY REALLY ARE.

It can be:

CHARACTER PARTICIPATES IN THE CONSTRUCTION OF THEIR OWN ACCOUNT.

This is an important anti-reduction device.

---

## 11. Source transformation as voice creation

Source comparison shows that Shakespeare often preserves narrative skeleton while redistributing dramatic standing.

### Hamlet
A relatively direct revenge structure becomes an interrogative drama whose protagonist's interior life and uncertainty dominate the action.

### King Lear
Multiple sources are recombined, including a secondary Gloucester plot, producing parallel voices and consequences rather than one simple moral line.

### Othello
A preexisting jealousy story becomes a tightly staged conflict among testimony, insinuation, trust, race, marriage, military hierarchy, and manipulated evidence.

The authorial operation may therefore include:

ADD POSITIONS
+
DISTRIBUTE KNOWLEDGE
+
INCREASE CONSEQUENCE OF INTERPRETATION.

This should be tested play by play rather than universalized.

---

## 12. Receiver operation

The Shakespearean audience must:
- listen across voices,
- remember who knows what,
- detect irony,
- hold competing motives,
- compare public and private speech,
- watch words become actions,
- revise allegiance,
- judge without guaranteed authorial resolution.

In some plays the audience knows more than a character.

In others the audience remains uncertain too.

The receiver therefore performs:

MULTI-POSITIONAL ATTENTION.

This is stronger than passive empathy.

---

## 13. Authorship locus

Agency is distributed differently at different levels.

### Textual / compositional
Primarily Shakespeare in many plays, with collaboration in some.

### Inherited
Sources, genres, prior plays, historical narratives.

### Realization
Actors, company practices, stage space, timing, bookkeeper, performance.

### Constraint
Master of the Revels, patronage, legal and commercial theater conditions.

### Deposit
Printers, stationers, Heminge and Condell, later editors.

The important finding is:

A STRONGLY INDIVIDUAL AUTHOR CAN STILL CREATE THROUGH A DISTRIBUTED ECOLOGY OF REALIZATION.

This is different from both Homer and the Biblical corpus.

---

## 14. Authorial office

Existing thesis language:

RELEASE OF INDEPENDENT DRAMATIC VOICE.

More precise provisional office:

DRAMATIC CONDITION-MAKER.

Alternative:

HOST OF VOICES.

The first is stronger because Shakespeare does not merely host speech.

He:
- selects the source,
- establishes conflict,
- distributes information,
- sets entrances and exits,
- gives characters speech,
- creates consequences,
- determines much of the formal field.

Yet he withholds continuous interpretive sovereignty inside that field.

Working definition:

A DRAMATIC CONDITION-MAKER DESIGNS THE CONDITIONS UNDER WHICH MULTIPLE VOICES CAN ACT WITHOUT REDUCING THEIR LOCAL STANDING TO A SINGLE EXPLICIT AUTHORIAL PROPOSITION.

Keep provisional.

---

## 15. Outside / independent standing

Several forms of independent standing matter.

### Actor
Another person must embody the role.
The author cannot fully determine every future performance.

### Audience
Reception can resist intended effects.

### Theater company
Material capacities and repertory needs constrain what can be staged.

### Censor
The Master of the Revels can require alteration or withhold license.

### Source
Inherited stories already possess cultural standing before Shakespeare transforms them.

### Collaboration
Other playwrights may contribute actual textual material.

### Textual transmission
Quarto/Folio differences resist the fantasy of one transparently recoverable authorial final text in every play.

The dramatic work therefore exceeds private intention through both making and succession.

---

## 16. Failure mode

The Shakespearean operation has several shadows.

### CHARACTER AS MOUTHPIECE
Voice appears plural but every character merely confirms the author's thesis.

### FALSE AUTONOMY
The critic romanticizes character "independence" and forgets the author's construction.

### POLYPHONY WITHOUT CONSEQUENCE
Many voices coexist but nothing forces them into action or cost.

### AUTHORIAL DISAPPEARANCE AS EXCUSE
The author claims no responsibility because characters or performers "did it."

The constitutional seam is:

DESIGN
<->
LOCAL VOICE.

Enough design for consequence.

Enough local standing that the voice is not merely illustration.

---

## 17. Reception / continued formation

A Shakespeare play continues changing through performance.

Different actors can alter:
- sympathy,
- emphasis,
- tempo,
- motive,
- comic or tragic weight,
- relation among characters.

Different stages and historical moments can produce different dramatic events from substantially the same words.

This means reception can become:

RE-REALIZATION.

Unlike Biblical canon formation, reception does not necessarily change the text's canonical standing.

Unlike Homeric composition-in-performance, a modern performance does not usually claim to rewrite the inherited text as oral tradition.

But it can materially change the experienced work.

This deserves a new distinction:

TEXTUAL IDENTITY
vs
PERFORMANCE IDENTITY.

---

## 18. Deposit / succession

Shakespeare's deposit is strikingly non-solitary.

It includes:
- performances during his lifetime,
- quarto printing,
- company-held theatrical materials,
- the 1623 First Folio assembled after his death by Heminge and Condell,
- subsequent editorial traditions,
- centuries of reperformance.

Many plays survive because others chose to collect and print them.

Therefore:

THE AUTHOR MADE THE PLAYS.

OTHERS HELPED MAKE "SHAKESPEARE."

That sentence should be treated as a project compression, not a denial of Shakespeare's authorship.

---

## 19. Surviving operation

The strongest current extraction is:

DESIGN A FIELD
-> DISTRIBUTE PARTIAL KNOWLEDGE
-> GIVE POSITIONS EMBODIED VOICES
-> LET SPEECH BECOME ACTION
-> FORCE CONSEQUENCE
-> WITHHOLD FINAL AUTHORIAL ADJUDICATION
-> REQUIRE THE RECEIVER TO JUDGE AMONG VOICES.

Compressed:

CONDITION
-> VOICE
-> COLLISION
-> CONSEQUENCE
-> RECEIVER JUDGMENT.

---

## 20. THEN -> NOW

The wrong contemporary equivalent would be:
- Shakespearean diction,
- five-act imitation,
- blank verse as costume,
- modern characters with Elizabethan speech,
- AI-generated monologues pretending to be Shakespeare.

The deeper contemporary pressure concerns authorship in systems that can generate many voices.

AI can now produce:
- multiple characters,
- arguments,
- styles,
- synthetic viewpoints,
- simulated dialogue.

But simulated plurality can still be one system talking to itself.

The Shakespearean pressure therefore intensifies:

HOW DO WE CREATE MULTIPLE VOICES WITH REAL LOCAL STANDING RATHER THAN MERELY GENERATING MANY OUTPUTS FROM ONE AUTHORIAL SYSTEM?

A contemporary equivalent may require voices that are constrained by:
- independent people,
- real evidence,
- durable character history,
- consequences,
- commitments the central system cannot silently rewrite.

---

## 21. Contemporary question

Can a contemporary masterwork design the conditions for many voices without converting those voices into puppets — and can the author remain responsible for the field without claiming ownership of everything that happens inside it?
