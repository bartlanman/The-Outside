# Shakespeare — Overview

## Unit of analysis

Primary unit:

SHAKESPEAREAN DRAMATIC PRACTICE

with selected plays used as test cases, especially:
- *Hamlet*
- *Othello*
- *King Lear*
- *Macbeth*
- *The Tempest*
- selected histories and comedies where useful

This is not a claim that all Shakespearean plays operate identically.

The dossier studies the recurring authorial problem already identified in the thesis sequence:

RELEASE OF DRAMATIC VOICE.

## Existing role in The Outside constellation

- Shakespeare as witness for authorship that creates conditions without collapsing every character into a single authorial proposition
- *The Tempest* as analogue for the Great Architect: condition-setting, control, relinquishment
- selected plays as evidence that an author can create voices with strong local standing inside a designed dramatic field

## Spine question

How does an author design a dramatic field strongly enough to produce consequence while withholding enough authorial adjudication that characters can appear to think, desire, justify, misread, and act from positions not reducible to a single thesis?

## Why Shakespeare matters after Homer and the Biblical corpus

Homer:
authorship can be distributed through carrying and recomposition.

Biblical corpus:
authorship can occur through custody and relation among inherited voices.

Shakespeare adds:

AN AUTHOR CAN DELIBERATELY CREATE A FIELD IN WHICH VOICES ARE GIVEN EMBODIED LOCAL AGENCY.

The important word is not "independence" in a metaphysical sense.

The characters remain authored.

The question is how the form prevents them from functioning merely as mouthpieces.
