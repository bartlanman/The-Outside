# Shakespeare — Evidence Notes

## Archive condition

Status:

LOW FOR AUTHORIAL DRAFTS
RICHER FOR THEATRICAL, PRINT, COMPANY, AND SOURCE CONTEXT.

No securely authenticated authorial manuscript of a Shakespeare play survives.

The major possible exception is "Hand D" in the collaborative manuscript of *Sir Thomas More*, which many scholars consider probably Shakespeare's handwriting, but the attribution remains debatable because the only handwriting securely known to be Shakespeare's consists of six signatures.

No manuscript copies of Shakespeare's works are known to survive with his authority or from a securely identified close-circle copy.

Status:
DOCUMENTED / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION.

## Printed textual witnesses

Many plays survive in early quartos, the 1623 First Folio, or both.

Some quarto and Folio versions differ substantially.

Examples:
- *Hamlet* survives in markedly different early printed versions.
- *Othello* appears in a 1622 quarto and a different 1623 Folio text, with no scholarly consensus fully explaining all differences.
- *Henry IV, Part 2* contains Folio-only passages whose history has generated debate about theatrical cutting versus authorial revision.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

Research implication:

"THE SHAKESPEARE TEXT" is sometimes a later editorial stabilization of variant early witnesses.

## Theater company

Shakespeare was:
- playwright,
- actor,
- shareholder in the Lord Chamberlain's Men / King's Men,
- part-owner of the Globe.

The company became the King's Men under James I.

This places Shakespeare unusually close to:
- casting,
- repertory,
- performance,
- company economics,
- audience response,
- stage constraints.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Actors

Richard Burbage is strongly associated with major Shakespeare roles including:
- Richard III
- Hamlet
- Othello
- King Lear

Recent scholarship emphasizes a creative relationship between Shakespeare's writing and Burbage's capacities as a performer.

Other known company actors, including William Kemp and later Robert Armin, shaped the available comic and clowning possibilities.

Status:
ACTOR-ROLE LINKS PARTLY DOCUMENTED; SPECIFIC CLAIMS ABOUT WRITING FOR AN ACTOR RANGE FROM STRONG INFERENCE TO SPECULATION.

## Performance medium

The plays were written for professional performance, not merely private reading.

Theater scholarship stresses that drama reaches completion through:
- playwright
- company / actors
- stage
- spectators.

The Globe, Blackfriars, court performance, touring, and other venues created different performance conditions.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Gendered casting

Women did not perform on the professional public stage in Shakespeare's company; male actors and boys played female roles.

This materially conditions the original embodiment of characters such as Desdemona, Lady Macbeth, Rosalind, Viola, Cordelia, Cleopatra, and others.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Patronage and legal condition

Professional players required aristocratic or royal patronage.

Shakespeare's company operated first under the Lord Chamberlain and later under King James I.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Censorship / licensing

The Master of the Revels licensed plays for performance and could require changes.

The surviving *Sir Thomas More* manuscript contains censorship marks by Edmund Tilney.

The 1606 Act to Restrain Abuses of Players also affected religious language in performance and textual transmission.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Collaboration

Early modern commercial theater made collaboration common.

Shakespeare is now widely understood to have collaborated on some plays.

Examples with significant scholarly support include:
- *Henry VIII* with John Fletcher
- *The Two Noble Kinsmen* with Fletcher
- likely collaborative involvement in *Timon of Athens*
- possible contribution to *Sir Thomas More*

Specific boundaries between hands can remain contested.

Status:
COLLABORATION STRONGLY SUPPORTED IN PART OF THE CANON.

## Sources and inherited plots

Shakespeare regularly transformed existing materials rather than inventing plots ex nihilo.

Major source fields include:
- Holinshed's *Chronicles*
- Plutarch's *Lives*
- Cinthio
- Saxo Grammaticus / Belleforest
- earlier English plays
- Sidney's *Arcadia*
- classical myth and history
- chronicle and romance traditions

Examples:
- *Hamlet* inherits an older revenge story through Saxo, Belleforest, and a lost earlier English *Hamlet* tradition.
- *King Lear* draws on Holinshed, the earlier *King Leir*, Sidney, Spenser, and other materials.
- *Othello* closely follows a tale by Cinthio while transforming characterization and dramatic structure.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Repertory pressure

Early modern professional companies required a continuing supply of plays.

Scholarship on Elizabethan/Jacobean theater emphasizes:
- speed of production,
- revision,
- adaptation,
- collaboration,
- actor resources,
- competition,
- repertory needs.

Status:
STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS.

## First Folio

The 1623 First Folio was prepared after Shakespeare's death by fellow actors John Heminge and Henry Condell with publishers.

It gathered thirty-six plays and was decisive for preservation of many works that had not previously appeared in print.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

Research implication:

Shakespeare's deposit was partly made by others after his death.

## Major caution

Do not claim that Shakespeare's characters are literally independent of Shakespeare.

"Independent voice" is a PROJECT DESCRIPTION OF FORMAL EFFECT.

The historical evidence supports:
- distributed speech,
- actor embodiment,
- conflicting character positions,
- source transformation,
- company realization,
- absence of a continuous authorial narrator.

The stronger claim that a character "escapes the author" is interpretive and must remain so.
