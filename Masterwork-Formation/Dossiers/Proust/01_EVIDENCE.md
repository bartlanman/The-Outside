# Proust — Evidence Notes

This file records source-supported evidence separately from project interpretation.

## Evidentiary survivability

The Proust archive is exceptionally rich.

The Bibliothèque nationale de France preserves the great majority of Proust's manuscripts, including:
- preparatory notebooks
- 75 school notebooks / cahiers central to the genesis of the novel
- approximately 8,000 autograph pages in that cahier corpus
- clean-copy notebooks
- corrected typescripts
- galleys and proofs
- paperoles: pasted strips carrying additions
- work lists and reminders
- correspondence relevant to composition and publication

Other scholarship counts more than 100 notebooks related to the *Recherche* when different notebook categories are included.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Early form

In 1908 Proust began planning a substantial work associated with an argument against the critic Sainte-Beuve.

The project initially took the form of criticism / dialogue. Narrative material increasingly entered it.

BnF genetic work shows the essay project transforming into fiction; one notebook stage preserves a moment when many novelistic possibilities were still open.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Multiple openings and fragmentary composition

BnF genetic scholarship records numerous different openings to *Contre Sainte-Beuve*. Proust composed in relatively short sequences, rewrote passages repeatedly, used notebooks from both directions, added disconnected sketches, and later constructed links among fragments.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Earlier works and inheritance

Before the *Recherche*, Proust had:
- published *Les Plaisirs et les jours*
- drafted and abandoned *Jean Santeuil*
- translated and commented on Ruskin
- written journalism, criticism, and pastiches
- participated intensely in Parisian social life
- engaged publicly with the Dreyfus Affair

These activities supplied forms, voices, social observation, aesthetic problems, and material later transformed in the novel.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Ruskin and voice

Proust's engagement with John Ruskin was sustained from roughly 1900 to 1906. Cambridge scholarship treats the Ruskin translations and later pastiches as important stages in Proust's search for form and voice.

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

## Family, loss, health

Proust suffered serious asthma from childhood.

His father died in 1903.

His mother Jeanne Proust died in September 1905. Cambridge biographical work records a period of depression and illness following her death.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

Caution:
The importance of these facts to the final form of the *Recherche* must not be reduced to simple biography. Proust's own anti-Sainte-Beuve argument warns against treating the social / biographical person as a sufficient explanation of the work.

## Beginning of sustained composition

BnF and Cambridge chronologies place sustained work on "Combray" in 1909 and the larger genesis of the novel in 1908–1909.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Publication pressure

The first volume was rejected by multiple publishers.

Proust eventually contracted with Bernard Grasset and published *Du côté de chez Swann* at his own expense in 1913.

Grasset considered the book too long and required cuts; displaced material was later reused.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Proofs as writing space

Corrected proofs show Proust continuing to add, revise, and expand after typesetting. Paperoles physically extend manuscript pages.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## World War I and expansion

At the outbreak of World War I, Proust had published the first volume and conceived the work in a much shorter architecture, commonly described as a trilogy.

The war interrupted publication and became part of the novel's historical material. Scholarship describes the *Recherche* as we know it as materially shaped by the war period.

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION.

## Agostinelli

Alfred Agostinelli, who had been close to Proust, died in an airplane crash in 1914.

Proust's later composition of the Albertine material is biographically entangled with this loss, but simple one-to-one identification of Albertine with Agostinelli is contested and should be avoided.

Status: DOCUMENTED event; literary transformation requires careful scholarship.

## Ending / architecture

Genetic evidence suggests that major end-material connected with time, recognition, and the final social gathering existed early, while the middle of the work expanded substantially over years.

Status: DOCUMENTED in broad outline.

## Late incompletion

Proust died in November 1922 while still revising. The final volumes were established and published posthumously by his brother Robert Proust and the NRF team.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Reception

An early publisher's report complained that the manuscript was extremely long, difficult, and obscure, yet also recognized an extraordinary intellectual phenomenon.

*À l'ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs* won the Prix Goncourt in 1919.

The work's prose and scale appeared radical to many contemporary readers and later became central to European modernism.

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS.
