## Major scholarly orientation

### Nathalie Mauriac Dyer
"Composition and publication of *À la recherche du temps perdu*," in *Marcel Proust in Context*, Cambridge University Press, 2013.

Key use:
manuscript evidence, composition chronology, publication history, surviving archive.

### William C. Carter
"Life," in *Marcel Proust in Context*, Cambridge University Press, 2013.

Key use:
biographical chronology, health, family, emergence of the long work.

### Marion Schmid
"The birth and development of *À la recherche du temps perdu*," in *The Cambridge Companion to Proust*.

Key use:
fourteen-year compositional development and genetic stages.

### Brigitte Mahuzier
"The First World War," in *Marcel Proust in Context*.

Key use:
the war's material role in the expansion and final form of the novel.

### Cynthia Gamble
"Finding a voice: from Ruskin to the pastiches," in *Marcel Proust in Context*.

Key use:
voice formation, Ruskin, imitation, Proust's sensitivity to authorial rhythm.

### Edward J. Hughes
"The Dreyfus Affair," in *Marcel Proust in Context*.

Key use:
political involvement and later retrospective transformation of the Affair in fiction.

### David Ellison
*A Reader's Guide to Proust's In Search of Lost Time*, Cambridge University Press.

Key use:
style as vision, syntax, relation between *Contre Sainte-Beuve* and the novel.

## Evidence cautions

### Biography
Do not convert a real person directly into a fictional character without scholarship.

In particular:
- Albertine != simply Agostinelli
- narrator != simply Proust
- Combray != transparent autobiography

Proust's own anti-Sainte-Beuve position makes biographical reduction especially methodologically inappropriate.

### Final volumes
Proust died before completing final revision. Posthumous textual establishment by Robert Proust and the NRF team must remain part of the deposit / succession problem.

### Archive abundance
Abundant drafts do not automatically make authorial intention transparent.

More evidence can reveal greater instability rather than greater certainty.
