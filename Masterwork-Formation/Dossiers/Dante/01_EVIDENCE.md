# Dante — Evidence Notes

This file records evidence and uncertainty before project interpretation.

## Evidentiary survivability
No autograph manuscript of the *Commedia* survives. This sharply limits reconstruction of drafts, rejected structures, alternate endings, and line-level compositional decisions.

Status: DOCUMENTED LIMITATION.

## Major inheritances
Strongly attested inheritances include:
- Scripture
- Virgil
- Aristotle
- scholastic philosophy and theology
- classical poetry
- vernacular lyric traditions
- Florentine civic and political life

Status: DOCUMENTED / STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS.

## Historical pressure
Dante participated in Florentine civic and political life and was exiled after the Black Guelf takeover. He never returned to Florence.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Personal / positional pressure
Exile changed Dante's literal relation to Florence and widened his political and linguistic horizon. His writings support the claim that he increasingly thought beyond the authority of his native city.

Status: DOCUMENTED, with causal significance to the *Commedia* still requiring inference.

## Audience and language
Dante chose the vernacular for an intellectually and theologically ambitious poem, thereby addressing readers beyond the learned Latin elite.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Material situation
The work was composed in exile within manuscript culture, under conditions of patronage and mobility among Italian courts.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Formal structure
Strongly documented features include:
- three cantiche
- one hundred cantos
- terza rima
- tightly patterned numerical architecture
- sequential passage through Inferno, Purgatorio, Paradiso
- a first-person traveler/narrator structure

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Reader relation
The poem repeatedly addresses and recruits the reader into acts of interpretation and attention.

Status: TEXTUALLY DOCUMENTED.

## Refusal / return
A traditionally attributed letter records Dante refusing a humiliating route back to Florence.

Status: TEXTUAL EVIDENCE WITH ATTRIBUTION CAUTION.

## Reception
The poem circulated rapidly in manuscript culture. Commentary began very early, within years of Dante's death.

Status: DOCUMENTED.

## Major gaps
We cannot securely reconstruct:
- full draft history
- rejected endings
- abandoned architectures
- exact sequence of all compositional decisions
- all editorial or scribal interventions in the author's lifetime

These gaps must remain gaps.
