# Eliot — Overview

## Unit of analysis

Primary work:
T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land*.

Secondary conceptual text:
"Tradition and the Individual Talent" (1919).

Supporting context:
- Eliot's early poetry
- Ezra Pound's editorial intervention
- Vivien Eliot's comments and revisions
- *The Criterion*
- the poem's Notes and publication history

## Existing role in The Outside constellation

- Eliot as witness for curation of inheritance
- *The Waste Land* as analogue for Bankruptcy: inherited symbolic assets remain after confidence in a coherent order has weakened
- *Four Quartets* elsewhere as a masterwork of time and possibility

This dossier focuses on the authorship-spine question:

CAN AUTHORSHIP RESIDE IN SELECTION, JUXTAPOSITION, CUTTING, AND REWEIGHTING OF INHERITED MATERIAL?

## Spine question

How can a poet make a new work from fragments, quotations, voices, languages, myths, prior texts, popular speech, and editorial intervention — and where does curation become canon-making, appropriation, or concealed collaboration?

## Why Eliot matters after Whitman

Whitman expands the authorial self to contain the many.

Eliot attempts almost the opposite move:

REDUCE THE EXPLICIT AUTHORIAL SELF
AND
LET INHERITED MATERIALS COLLIDE.

But the curator does not disappear.

Someone still chooses:
- which fragments enter,
- what gets cut,
- which traditions count,
- which relation becomes visible,
- what remains untranslated,
- what the reader is expected to recognize.
