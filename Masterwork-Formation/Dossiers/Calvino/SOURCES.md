# Calvino — Sources

## Primary authorial statements

### Italo Calvino, "The Written and the Unwritten Word"
*The New York Review of Books*, May 12, 1983.
Translated by William Weaver.

Use:
- written page versus multiform external world
- insistence on testing whether world remains irreducible to words
- writing as movement between page and outside
- *If on a winter's night a traveler* beginning from forms Calvino felt unable to write
- ten imaginary novelists
- writing as self-change

https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1983/05/12/the-written-and-the-unwritten-word/

### Paris Review, "The Art of Fiction No. 130"
1992 publication of late Calvino interview.

Use:
- Calvino's retrospective description of the highly articulated structure of *If on a winter's night a traveler*
- distinction between hidden structural labor and readerly pleasure

https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2019/10/22/redux-cold-night-of-october/

### Italo Calvino, "Cybernetics and Ghosts"
Lecture delivered 1967.

Use:
- literature as combinatorial game
- possibility of writing machines
- distinction between permutation and the unexpected meaning produced in relation to historically situated consciousness
- "ghosts" around the machine

Collected in *The Literature Machine* / *The Uses of Literature*.

## Evidence cautions

Do not state:
- that Oulipo means random generation;
- that Calvino eliminates human authorship;
- that a writing machine automatically produces literature;
- that the fictional Reader is identical with the actual reader;
- that reader participation equals coauthorship;
- that second-person address guarantees agency;
- that Calvino "predicted ChatGPT" in any simple historical sense.

Distinguish:

GENERATION
from
MEANING.

Distinguish:

DESIGNED PARTICIPATION
from
PARTICIPANT INDEPENDENCE.

Distinguish:

RESPONSIVENESS
from
ABILITY TO ALTER CONDITIONS.

Distinguish:

FICTIONAL "YOU"
from
ACTUAL RECEIVER.
