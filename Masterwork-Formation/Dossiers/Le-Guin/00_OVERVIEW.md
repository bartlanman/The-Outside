# Le Guin — Overview

## Unit of analysis

Primary corpus:
Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea cycle across two major creative phases:

Early Earthsea:
- *A Wizard of Earthsea* (1968)
- *The Tombs of Atuan* (1971)
- *The Farthest Shore* (1972)

Return to Earthsea:
- *Tehanu* (1990)
- *Tales from Earthsea* (2001)
- *The Other Wind* (2001)
- later Earthsea stories and authorial essays

Supporting formation materials:
- "Earthsea Revisioned"
- later afterwords and forewords
- interviews
- correspondence
- archival manuscript evidence
- Le Guin's reflections on feminism, power, fantasy, and revision

## Existing role in The Outside constellation

Le Guin has already appeared as one of the strongest possible analogues for:

AUTHOR_0
-> WORLD_0
-> TIME / ENCOUNTER
-> AUTHOR_1
-> RETURN TO WORLD
-> REVISION WITHOUT ERASURE.

She also provides:
- mature power as restraint,
- teaching rather than domination,
- balance rather than mastery,
- return to a created world from another position,
- authorial development without disowning prior work.

## Spine question

What happens when an author returns decades later to a world she created and discovers that the world now requires her to revise assumptions she herself originally placed there?

## Why Le Guin matters after Tolkien

Tolkien gives us:

WORLD EXCEEDS PARTICIPANT.

Le Guin adds:

WORLD CAN ALSO EXCEED THE AUTHOR'S EARLIER UNDERSTANDING OF IT.

The author may return not as sovereign owner but as a later participant in a world whose earlier conditions now answer back.
