# Biblical Corpus — Overview

## Unit of analysis

This dossier does not treat "the Bible" as one book made by one author.

The unit is:

BIBLICAL CORPUS FORMATION

including, where relevant:
- composition
- oral and scribal inheritance
- supplementation
- redaction
- textual transmission
- translation
- collection
- communal use
- canon formation
- later stabilization

Primary focus:
- Hebrew Bible / Jewish Scriptures
- early Christian use of Jewish Scriptures
- New Testament collections and canon formation

Important caution:

There is no single historically universal "Bible" with one uncontested table of contents and order.

Jewish, Roman Catholic, Protestant, Eastern Orthodox, and other Christian traditions preserve related but non-identical canons.

## Existing role in The Outside constellation

The Bible serves as the primary masterwork analogue for CORPUS:

distinct writings become mutually interpretive without becoming identical.

It also serves the authorship spine as a model of:
- layered custody
- redaction
- collection
- canon
- succession across communities

## Spine question

How can writings produced in different periods, genres, communities, and authorial situations acquire a shared corpus identity — and how does collection itself change the standing and interpretation of the writings without erasing their local difference?

## Why this case matters after Homer

Homer showed that authorship can be distributed through tradition, performance, transmission, and stabilization.

Biblical formation adds another operation:

BOUNDARY-MAKING.

The tradition does not merely carry material forward.

It also:
- edits,
- juxtaposes,
- translates,
- collects,
- recognizes,
- excludes,
- orders,
- and gives some writings durable standing in relation to others.

Preliminary finding:

CORPUS FORMATION IS NOT NEUTRAL ACCUMULATION.

IT IS A FORM OF RELATIONAL AUTHORSHIP.
