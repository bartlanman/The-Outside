## Hebrew Bible as scribal formation

Recent scholarship emphasizes the role of Israelite and Judahite scribal culture in the formation and growth of biblical literature.

Biblical tradition did not merely preserve earlier material; scribes composed, recorded, archived, adapted, supplemented, and transformed received material.

Status:
STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS ON SCRIBAL CULTURE.
SPECIFIC LAYERING MODELS REMAIN DEBATED BOOK BY BOOK.

## Source / form / redaction criticism

Modern biblical scholarship uses source, form, tradition, redaction, literary, and canonical approaches to reconstruct how texts may contain earlier units, sources, oral traditions, and later editorial shaping.

Redaction criticism specifically studies editorial shaping and the relation of prior material to larger literary wholes.

Status:
ESTABLISHED SCHOLARLY METHODS.
PARTICULAR RECONSTRUCTIONS ARE HYPOTHESES, NOT DIRECTLY OBSERVED DRAFT HISTORY.

## Pentateuch

Traditional Jewish and Christian interpretation associated the Torah / Pentateuch with Moses.

Modern scholarship generally treats Pentateuchal composition as historically complex and debates the number, character, and dating of sources, supplements, and redactions.

No single modern compositional model has universal scholarly acceptance.

Status:
COMPLEX COMPOSITION STRONGLY SUPPORTED.
SPECIFIC MODELS CONTESTED.

## Textual plurality

The earliest Hebrew evidence shows that biblical books existed in multiple textual forms in antiquity.

The Dead Sea Scrolls, Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, and later Masoretic tradition provide evidence of textual plurality and transmission.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Jewish canon formation

The formation of the Jewish canon was a historical process.

Recent scholarship cautions against imagining one universally fixed official canon at an early date.

Authoritative collections and scriptural groupings existed before later definitive evidence of a fixed canon.

Timothy Lim argues that before roughly 100 CE different Jewish communities could recognize differing collections of authoritative scripture.

James VanderKam likewise notes that definitive surviving evidence for a Jewish canon comes later than the earliest evidence for authoritative books and collections.

Status:
STRONG SCHOLARLY SUPPORT FOR GRADUAL FORMATION AND EARLIER PLURALITY.
DETAILS AND DATING DEBATED.

## Septuagint

The Greek translation of Jewish Scriptures originated in Jewish contexts and later became deeply important in Christianity.

Its language and textual forms influenced early Christian scripture use and New Testament authors.

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## New Testament collections

Early Christian writings initially circulated as individual or smaller collections.

By the second century:
- collections of Pauline letters were widely known;
- a fourfold Gospel collection had substantial standing.

Other writings remained disputed longer.

Status:
STRONG SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS.

## New Testament canon formation

The New Testament canon formed gradually.

Important factors discussed by scholarship include:
- connection with apostolic figures or the apostolic church
- perceived harmony with Christian teaching
- widespread use and reception in Christian communities

Later fourth-century canon lists clarified boundaries, but some regional variation continued.

Status:
DOCUMENTED PROCESS / DETAILS DEBATED.

## Canon diversity

Different Jewish and Christian traditions ultimately preserve related but different biblical canons, including differences in:
- contents
- order
- status of deuterocanonical / anagignoskomena writings
- relation of Hebrew and Greek textual traditions

Status:
DOCUMENTED.

## Textual transmission continues

Both Hebrew Bible and New Testament textual history continued long after initial composition.

Copyists, translators, readers, and editors sometimes preserved, altered, clarified, harmonized, or transmitted variant readings.

Modern textual criticism often speaks not of recovering one transparent autograph in every case but of reconstructing earliest attainable or initial textual states from surviving witnesses.

Status:
DOCUMENTED / CURRENT TEXT-CRITICAL PRACTICE.
