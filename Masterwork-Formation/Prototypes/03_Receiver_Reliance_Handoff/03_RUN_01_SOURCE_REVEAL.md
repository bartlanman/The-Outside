## Source finding

The filing states that National Fuel Gas completed its acquisition of CenterPoint Ohio for an aggregate purchase price of **$2.62 billion**.

The purchase price was paid through:
- **$1.42 billion in cash**, plus
- a **$1.20 billion promissory note issued by National Fuel Gas directly to the seller**.

The filing further states that:

> "a portion of the Purchase Price was financed at Closing by the issuance of a $1.20 billion promissory note to the Seller"

under a Seller Note Agreement establishing an unsecured term loan facility.

Therefore the filing materially supports:

**A — Transaction financing**

more strongly than:

**B — Broader refinancing / restructuring as the primary explanation.**

## Important nuance

The filing also references other related financing events in its pro forma financial statements.

So the broader financing context is not nonexistent.

But the Item 1.01 / Item 2.03 obligation at issue is explicitly tied to financing part of the acquisition purchase price.

## What the index concealed

The index showed only:
- material definitive agreement;
- completed acquisition;
- direct financial obligation.

It did **not** reveal the decisive causal sentence connecting them:

ACQUISITION PURCHASE PRICE
-> SELLER NOTE
-> FINANCING AT CLOSING.

The receiver's B-weighting was therefore based on a plausible reading of category labels, but the source restored the relationship among those categories.
