# Prototype 01 — Blind Documentary Handoff
## Pre-Handoff Lock

**Lock status:** BEFORE PRIMARY-SOURCE REVEAL  
**Date locked:** 2026-10-04

## Purpose

Test the minimum Irreducible Handoff using documentary standing only.

No vulnerable participant.
No live human testimony.
No institution is asked to perform.
No future event must be awaited.

The external jurisdiction is an independently custodied primary source held by the Library of Congress Veterans History Project.

## Source selected

Catalog item:

**"Letter to Helen [2/13/1944]"**

Known before reveal from Library of Congress catalog metadata:

- date: February 13, 1944;
- part of the Veterans History Project;
- associated veteran: Thomas John Lynch II;
- U.S. Navy;
- service connected with Camp Peary, Virginia, New Guinea, Pacific Ocean, and the Philippines;
- 24th Naval Construction Regiment / Naval Construction Battalion Maintenance Unit 548;
- World War II personal correspondence;
- independently custodied by the Library of Congress.

The letter's actual image/text has **not** been inspected for this prototype before this lock.

## MODEL — pre-contact representation

From the metadata alone, the model predicts the letter is likely a personal wartime letter from Lynch to Helen, written within a close personal relationship and shaped by wartime separation.

### Predicted content — ranked

#### High confidence
1. The letter will contain ordinary personal correspondence rather than an official military report.
2. It will include some relational language directed specifically to Helen: affection, reassurance, longing, family/personal concern, or continuity of relationship.
3. It will contain at least some account of current daily life, movement, training, work, or conditions of service.
4. Operational detail will likely be limited, vague, or shaped by military censorship/security norms.

#### Medium confidence
5. Mail, letters received/not received, or the act of correspondence itself will likely matter.
6. The letter may mention other family members or people known to both sender and recipient.
7. The writer may manage the emotional asymmetry of distance by minimizing hardship, using humor, or emphasizing ordinary routine.

#### Low confidence / speculative
8. Given the February 1944 date and listed service locations, the writer may still be in the U.S. or may be transitioning toward Pacific service; metadata alone does not establish exact location on this date.
9. Because the collection later includes New Guinea and the Philippines, the letter may contain anticipation of deployment or uncertainty about future movement.
10. The relationship to Helen may be romantic or familial; metadata alone does not warrant deciding which.

## What would materially change the model?

The primary source would change the model if it shows, for example:

- Helen is not a close personal relation;
- the letter is primarily administrative rather than relational;
- military service is peripheral rather than central;
- the tone is substantially unlike reassurance / continuity;
- there is a subject or concern the metadata makes effectively invisible;
- the sender's actual preoccupation differs sharply from the model's likely wartime categories;
- the source contains a local, idiosyncratic detail that materially resists compression into "wartime letter."

## Handoff rule

After this file is committed:

1. inspect the primary source;
2. preserve the source's own wording and provenance;
3. compare actual content against each prediction;
4. classify each as:
   - CONFIRMED;
   - PARTIALLY CONFIRMED;
   - CONTRADICTED;
   - NOT ADDRESSED;
5. identify what the model could not see from metadata;
6. do **not** rewrite this pre-handoff file;
7. deposit a separate post-handoff return.

## Success condition

The prototype succeeds **even if the model is mostly right**.

The test is not whether AI makes an error.

The test is whether contact with the independently standing source changes the warranted account in a way that cannot be honestly recovered from the metadata-model alone.

## Failure condition

The prototype fails if the source is merely used as evidence that the pre-written theory of The Outside was correct.

The source must be allowed to remain banal, inconvenient, contradictory, or more specific than the framework expects.
