# HUM — FELT INTENSITY / THE READER MUST FEEL IT
**Status:** Active creative-direction extension; experimental / not canon.
**Purpose:** Prevent Hallucination from becoming a detached description of a climax. Generate embodied, coherent, increasingly intimate experience whose final release happens through rhythm, action, imagery and reception—not by asserting that a peak occurred.

## CENTRAL RULE
**DO NOT SAY THE HUM IS INTENSE. WRITE SO ITS PRESSURE BECOMES FELT.**
The artwork should change the reader's felt expectation through sentence movement, bodily specificity and the recurrence of charged details. Emotional and sensual intensity may become orgiastic in the broad artistic sense of collective abandon, rapture and transformation, without graphic sexual acts.

## FELT-SCORE: NOT A CHECKLIST IN THE STORY
1. **TOUCH THE ACTUAL CHARGE:** identify each character's own appetite, withheld gesture, tension, comic instability, fear or tenderness from BOTH original fields. Start from concrete things—a damp palm, catching breath, a collarbone under fabric, a tremor in a voice, a ledger edge, a vibrating rim—rather than generalized glowing energy.
2. **PROXIMITY / DANGEROUSLY CLOSE:** make distance, gaze, heat, trembling, interrupted speech, kisses, embraces and other consensual intimate gestures change relationships on the page. Allow erotic charge and sexiness without explicit sexual choreography. Characters must respond differently, not become an identical crowd.
3. **CRESCENDO THROUGH FORM:** vary the length, cadence and syntax of sentences; accelerate, interrupt, repeat transformed motifs, stop abruptly, and resume. Sensory consequence replaces theoretical vocabulary. Avoid explanatory interruption at the emotional peak.
4. **CASCADE OF RELEASE:** let laughter shift into crying, crying into voice, voice into shaking or intense stillness; different people may carry different releases. An orgasmic *quality* of culmination is available as metaphor or non-graphic depiction, not an obligation for every participant to have a literal sexual climax.
5. **ATTRACTIVE COHERENCE:** import the real-world Outside Substance Draw not as facts but as matter acting on the original two fields. Every unexpected transformation must arise from accumulated context and make the next movement more inevitable in hindsight.
6. **ONE WHOLE-FIELD PEAK:** the final peak gathers every character and charged object from both fields, including the unresolved and withheld. Not a list of five peaks; rather, each smaller release changes the total pressure until all movements resonate in one event.
7. **SCREEN CROSSING AS SENSORY TRANSDUCTION:** the final coherence vortex passes through the *fictional* monitor as a change of narrative medium. The reader encounters its transformed substance in imagination: perhaps a shape, breath, afterimage or sensation that was impossible before the two fields.
8. **AFTERPULSE:** quiet must also carry changed gravity. No clinical recap immediately after the crescendo; leave a resonant physical detail as the closing image.

## PROHIBITIONS AGAINST BORING HUM
- No office-by-office analysis in the narrative; no announcing “activation,” “integration,” “peak release,” or “coherence” as a substitute for depicting it.
- No generic crowd screaming inside an arbitrary neon cyclone. Give each individual a consequential gesture and let those gestures combine.
- Do not move every character through the same physiological response; intensities must vary and cross.
- Do not swap source-specific physical motifs for generic fantasy decoration.
- Never claim a story produces guaranteed reader arousal or measurable physiological effects; design for felt involvement, allow individual reception.
- No explicit pornographic acts, coercive participation, or minors in erotic contexts.

## REVISION TEST
Read only the continuous story with no prefatory explanation. Ask:
- Can the reader infer increasing pressure without being told?
- Does each movement emerge from two-field context?
- Is the erotic charge embodied, reciprocal and character-specific?
- Are releases actually dramatized in prose rather than named?
- Does the final gathering create a formal change in sentence, image and reader orientation?
- Does the final screen crossing leave an afterimage?
If not, rewrite the weak passage; do not add a paragraph explaining what it was meant to do.

**COMPRESSION:** SOURCE-SPECIFIC DESIRE → SENSORY PROXIMITY → RHYTHMIC ACCELERATION → DIFFERENT RELEASES → WHOLE-FIELD COHERENCE → THE SCREEN GIVES WAY → READER AFTERPULSE.
