## Why revise
Version 0.1 correctly protected difference and provenance but risked making preserved separation the required artistic outcome. DOS allows release of the form, including the experienced separation of self/other. **Guard the conditions for dissolution, not a compulsory separation or compulsory unity.**

## Four different claims (do not collapse)
1. **Musical unison:** pitches or waveforms converge; measurable signal feature, not identity of sources.
2. **Relational unity:** separate contributions form a coherent encounter; not necessarily unison.
3. **Experiential dissolution:** a witness may report fading subject/object or self/other distinction; report as phenomenology, not proof of external fusion.
4. **Ontological merger:** independent entities truly become one; neither musical unison nor narrative/subjective experience establishes this.

**UNITY IS ADMISSIBLE. DISSOLUTION IS ADMISSIBLE. DIFFERENCE IS ADMISSIBLE. NO ARRIVAL IS ADMISSIBLE.**

## Optional scored branch after HUM-04
Do not revise the inherited A/B entry and exit clocks. Fork a NEW, independently dated composition after the v0.1 reference has been played and source-locked.

- **Tension:** A/B relationships change against D; consonance and dissonance may both occur.
- **Ground:** allow audibly recoverable D or silence, and opt-out/STOP. Ground is not a new character.
- **Release:** let one or both voices stop pressing toward resolution.
- **Surrender:** the composition may relax its need to preserve distinguishable voices or the witness may cease actively sorting channels; report these separately.
- **Dissolution:** optional outcomes include audible unison; gradual spectral blending; fade into silence; abrupt cessation; continuing nonunison; or no event. Do not call them equivalent.
- **Re-entry:** let the witness listen again or make a new choice. Do not prescribe what form follows dissolution.

The original mystery channel remains unassigned. A synthetic common sound is made by identifiable software sources, not recovered evidence of a mysterious speaker.

## New inquiry: two histories, same terminal signal
The user observed that the full and simultaneous-cessation control can have sample-identical final D-only segments, even though their preceding passages differ. The signal alone cannot encode which passage occurred in its identical final segment. A witness who has heard the histories may perceive the ending differently. This is **historically conditioned reception**, not evidence that the field itself knows.

## Required tests for the new branch
- Blindly compare final identical excerpts alone versus full trajectories; separate measured signal difference, reported meaning, and guessed source.
- Include a **unison control** where A and B become the same pitch but retain independent synthesizer channels; test whether pitch convergence is wrongly heard as loss of source distinction.
- Include **silence** and **non-resolution** conditions; do not force satisfying release.
- Compare a **voluntary release** with an **abrupt cut**; assess distinct reception without declaring a winner.
- Ask for a phenomenological account of unity/dissolution, without prompting that answer.
- Use a new unfamiliar score for capacity transfer; no developmental claim before observable comparison.
