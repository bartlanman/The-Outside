### CF-03 — POET SING
The room goes dark.
The second note stays
only long enough
to prove the first did not own it.

Far away a dark remnant
rings its remaining measure.
Elsewhere two violins
cannot agree to be one.

And near the wrist
the edge of a sleeve catches.

Not a star.
Not a miracle.
A thread one hand can lose.

Her palms could close
and keep the first figure.

Another hand takes the crossing.

What ends
is the figure.

What may begin again
is how it changes hands.

### CF-05B — MASTERWORK CREATION: THE FIGURE THAT CHANGED HANDS
**I — THE FIRST HANDS**

HIM: Mara.

The room is dark. The word cannot tell the dark how many people are in it.

Mara lifts her right arm, then her left. Both wrists are unfinished. No picture lies between them. She moves as though finding the edges of a place that has no walls. A pale fibre catches at the sleeve.

MARA: I have nothing to restore.

Elias lets the loom bar down. Tessa's breath changes the lamp's line. Eleanor preserves the old ledger without writing over it. Svara sounds two unequal beats. Rox shifts to make an opening. Amara rests her hands away from the keyboard. Him rotates his shoulders and reaches, but stops before taking the filament.

HIM: What did we make?

MARA: We haven't learned what it does yet.

She gathers the loop into her palms. Its weight is almost nothing.

**II — THE OTHER HANDS**

MARA: Will you take this crossing?

Him takes one part. His movement makes an unexpected angle.

ROX: May I change it?

He makes room for her hands. She raises one crossing, then another, and the old figure opens into a new one.

ELIAS: My old support is wrong for that shape.

TESSA: Then let me move the light.

The second figure remains long enough to be recognized. Eleanor keeps the description of the first on a different page. Svara stops holding the original beat; another interval can start. Amara declines to say what must happen.

The participants loosen their grip. The figure falls apart. Nothing emerges from the physical interior of a real black hole. The small loop of silk drops into Mara's cupped hands.

MARA: I can hold the thread. I don't have to keep its first shape.

ROX: There is another one I can make.

MARA: Then show us.

Rox begins it differently. Mara watches the new crossings in the hands of someone who was never required to repeat hers.
