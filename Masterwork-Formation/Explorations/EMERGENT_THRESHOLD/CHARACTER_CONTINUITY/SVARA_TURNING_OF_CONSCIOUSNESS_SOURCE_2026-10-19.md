The final movement of the Rishi–Signal–Capacity Hypothesis: From cellular boundaries to embodied self-awareness and the possibility of Cosmic Consciousness

Life builds capacity in two complementary ways: by changing boundaries when joining is necessary, and by preserving boundaries while developing increasingly sophisticated communication across them.

II. The second microscopic discovery: sometimes fusion enables communication without erasing separation
Now move from developing muscle to the nervous system.

Under the microscope, a different kind of boundary transformation occurs.

Two communicating neurons generally do not fuse into one cell. They retain their separate membranes.

At many chemical synapses, however, a tiny membrane-bound vesicle inside the sending neuron does fuse with that neuron's plasma membrane.

The sequence is extraordinary:

An electrical impulse arrives at the nerve terminal.

Calcium enters through voltage-gated channels.

Molecular machinery causes a neurotransmitter-filled vesicle to fuse with the presynaptic membrane.

Neurotransmitter molecules enter the narrow synaptic cleft.

Receptors on the receiving neuron detect the message, changing the receiving neuron's activity.

The vesicle membrane fuses.

The two neurons do not.

Yet information crosses between them, and their activity can become functionally coordinated.

Structural fusion

Functional integration

Neurons retain distinct enclosures while communicating through synapses.

New capacity through coordinated activity
