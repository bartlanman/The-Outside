## CF-03 — SING (exact poetic content deposited here)
The hand came away
from the turning wood.
One vessel
surrendered its note.
Another, untouched,
began to drink the air.
Between their rims
a drop remained
unwilling to fall.
Two throats
held the same breath
for different lengths of time.
The first released.
The second
had not yet learned
that release was possible.
Bronze remembered
the hammer.
Water remembered
nothing of the blow.
A page was turned.
Its underside
kept the earlier hour.
The branch moved
after the wind had gone.
One mouth closed.
Farther in,
another was still
becoming capable
of its first sound.
No hand reached it.
No bell
had yet decided
which ringing
would be last.

## CF-06 — GRAPHIC PROMPT (EXACT LOCK)
An extraordinarily accomplished large-format mixed-media painting, nearly horizontal in composition, showing one continuous suspended band of folded handmade linen, hand-beaten pewter, and pale mineral plaster crossing a deep, quiet charcoal ground. The band occupies the lower and middle portions of the frame, sagging asymmetrically in two different rhythms; there is no central object, circle, portal, vessel, or dividing line. On the far left the band has already ceased moving: stiff, oxidized, cold, with a soft pleat that has settled into its last crease. Toward the lower right the same band continues to fold under an uncompleted gesture, its delicate material edge visibly displaced in successive, ghostlike registrations of motion. The two states coexist inside one unbroken material without an illustrated before-and-after split.

A sensual but non-explicit fragment of a clearly adult human body—side of neck, shoulder, collarbone, upper back—appears in the middle distance, intimately and partially continuous with the hanging material. Fine skin pores become linen fibrils at one boundary; at another, the body seems to carry a faint plaster impression of a motion that has stopped. No complete figure, no isolated hand, no erotic anatomical focus. The anatomy is structural, both bearing and receiving strain. The fabric pulls down with credible gravity and the human body retains distinct, soft living skin rather than becoming a cyborg.

At the rightmost continuing fold, a nearly invisible dry trace of graphite gathers in the crevice like the residue of a private act occurring after a more public motion has ended. Elsewhere, the last metallic ripple has settled into the pewter. The viewer must first perceive one sweeping gesture, then recognize that different segments have stopped at different times. The graphic must visually make an ending asymmetrical, not merely display two objects.

The upper half of the image is deliberately vast, empty, matte dark space with an almost imperceptible suspended dust-fall; it is a meaningful silence separating the terminated motion from the small persistent action. No glowing filaments, no mystical architecture, no giant landscape, no cyan-gold haze, no central icon. Meticulously worked encaustic, engraved drypoint and rough hand-laid tempera on fibrous linen, with visible erasures, irregular plate pressure, scratched pigment, and occasional oxidized metal leaf. Restrained palette of smoky graphite, ash, deep iron-grey, raw linen, umber skin tones, and near-black. Subtle raking light from an unseen low source, nothing spectacular or decorative. A visual event of differentiated duration, intimate physical consequence, and irreducible quiet. Absolutely no text, glyphs, letters, numbers, captions, maps, signs, or diagrams.
