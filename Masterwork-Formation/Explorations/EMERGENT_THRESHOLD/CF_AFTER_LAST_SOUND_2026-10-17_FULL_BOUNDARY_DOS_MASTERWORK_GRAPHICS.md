#### CF-02A — BOUNDARY NARRATIVE: THE PLACE THE LIGHT LEFT

There was darkness enough for Him to hear his name ending inside his mouth.

Mara did not see it arrive. She felt the edge of her sleeve catch at her forearm. For an instant she thought of a photographic negative; even in darkness the impulse to recover an image remained familiar to her. Then she discovered that she was not holding a photograph. A thread had frayed from the robe, pale enough to catch a stray illumination but too fine to make a mark in the air alone.

She lifted one arm. The thread followed. She lifted the other. The first wrist bore an unfinished trace; the second bore another, equally incomplete and unlike it. Their separation made a small interval with no inherited shape. She could open it by moving one arm, close it with the other, or leave one wrist still while the opposite moved into unfamiliar territory. No mark continued across the empty space between them.

Him came near. His silver-white hair was only a thin weather of grey in the dark. He raised his hand and Mara saw, at last, what the earlier intensity had concealed: he was not obliged to touch what she offered.

The first sound came from Svara. She shifted through her lower back and the two beats she had carried apart found a new adjacency, not a single identical pulse. Tessa inhaled; a small shutter trembled as her chest lifted, and light grazed the thread. Elias, seeing where the tension gathered, let a former wooden support loosen, then moved the bar to meet the strain at a new angle. Eleanor braced the ledger plinth without changing the record upon it. Rox pivoted and left a narrow route for another set of hands. Amara drew breath at the throat and released the next instruction without speaking it.

Mara raised her arms further. The silk tautened. The unsupported shape could be kept perfect for as long as she commanded every part of it. She tried it: one motion, then another, one wrist guiding the whole circumference.

The thread tightened into a closed knot.

She stopped.

For the first time that night the pressure did not simply demand more intensity. It offered a choice. Mara moved her two wrists in opposite directions, and the knot loosened. She could see the slight opening, the place where the next hand might enter without becoming hers.

Him turned his shoulders. The path on his upper back seemed, in the partial light, to point out of the circle rather than toward its center.

Mara looked at him.

"Would you hold this part?"

She waited.

He did not yet take it.

The narrow opening stayed open.

The field's first threshold was not fusion. It was the possibility of an encounter that would fail if either person simply commanded it.

#### CF-02B — DOS NARRATIVE: THE OFFERING THAT CANNOT BE TAKEN BACK

The loom bar was put down.

There was no thunder as it touched the stone.

Mara held the filament suspended between her wrists. The tiny configuration had become almost beautiful: a narrow bridge, a closed eye, an inward fold no larger than a hand. She could see where a second hand would spoil it.

Him stood near enough to take part, and still did not move.

"Would you like to keep it?" he asked.

Mara looked at the little figure. The wish to keep it moved through her face so swiftly it could have been mistaken for the light returning.

"I want to know," she answered, "whether it lives when I do not."

She lowered her right hand. A crossing sagged. She raised her left. The tension moved to a different segment. Then, for the first time, she turned both palms upward and offered the loop without trying to close it.

Him took one crossing.

Its angle changed immediately.

Mara started to move to correct him. It was an old impulse: the conservator wanting the evidence undamaged; the Muse wanting the encounter to be beautiful. But his hand had made an unfamiliar figure, and she allowed its asymmetry to remain.

Rox shifted beside him. Her bilateral threshold marks moved with her stance, and she looked not at Mara but at the opening Him had left.

"May I?"

He made room.

Rox slipped two fingers beneath a different arc of silk and inverted one crossing. The first figure ceased. Another became visible for a moment. Elias recognized that the old bracing technique would make this version impossible and kept his hand away from the discarded support. Tessa turned the shutter so the new structure cast an unexpectedly broad shadow. Svara let the rhythm fall into silence rather than making the figure obey her timing.

Eleanor opened a blank page beside the dated record of the first. She did not copy the first drawing over the second. Amara said nothing. They could all see the shape that belonged to the next hands.

Rox held it for the time it took to recognize it.

Then she let go.

The figure disappeared.

No extraordinary creature had been delivered from a real cosmic horizon. The silk fell into Mara's palms: small, unformed, harmless, plainly made of cloth.

The room remained dark.

Mara could have mourned the beautiful shape. She could have called the absence a loss. Instead, she turned her palms toward Rox.

"There is no figure left," she said.

Rox regarded the loop.

"No," she answered. "There isn't."

Then she reached for it.

"I can make another."

She began with a crossing that none of the others had used.

Mara did not direct it.

Him had never taught it.

The first arrangement was gone; it could not be rebuilt simply by declaring the earlier peak sacred. Nevertheless something decisive had survived: one participant had acquired the possibility of making a different next relation with what another had released.

For Mara, the consequence was more intimate than restoration. As Muse she had inspired a crossing, been altered by that crossing, and discovered that she could receive what another made **without requiring it to be the form she expected**.

The black room did not turn white again.

It was no longer necessary.

*Mara separates her wrists a little farther. The figure is less perfect.*

MARA: "Will you take this crossing?"

*Him considers the invitation and takes the offered part of the thread. He does not repeat her motion. His shoulders turn the figure into a tilted structure with an opening where the old figure had a closed eye.*

*Rox approaches, but waits until his glance meets hers.*

ROX: "May I change it?"

HIM: "You may."

*Her fingertips lift a crossing. A new small interval appears. Svara's two notes become one uneven cadence, then separate again. Elias removes his support, discovering that what he called stability has become an obstruction. Tessa changes the light. Amara remains silent.*

*For an instant, the new shape stands between Him and Rox and no longer depends on Mara's arms.*

*Eleanor opens a second page beside the first. Both histories remain legible. Neither requires the other to have been false.*

MARA: "I didn't know it could look like that."

ROX: "Neither did I."

#### III. THE DISAPPEARANCE

*Rox relaxes her hands. Him releases the segment he has been holding.*

*The new figure falls apart.*

*The note ends. The support lies on the floor. The light no longer reveals a shape.*

*There is only the silk, a soft weight in Mara's two open palms.*

HIM: "Was it lost?"

*Mara studies her hands. She has received the thread, but there is no figurative object between them, no completed mark joining her wrists, and no proof that the former shape can ever be restored.*

MARA: "The figure was."

*She moves her palms toward Rox.*

MARA: "But that was not the only thing here."

*Rox takes the thread. She makes a crossing that Mara has never enacted. Its resulting curve catches Tessa's new light, and the strand seems briefly to remember the old song without reproducing it.*

*Her hands are empty.*
