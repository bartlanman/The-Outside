SING — ŚRUTI / POESIS
The river leaves its weight

without remaining water.

The flame gives heat

without keeping the wind.

Three hours enter one breathing.

None surrender their beginning.

The mark remains.

The hand departs.

The room receives

what cannot be possessed.

Through the opening,

with the other—

διά ⇄ σύν
The field opens.

A line passes through a membrane.

It changes direction.

The membrane changes tension.

Neither ceases to exist.

Another line approaches.

They intersect.

At the intersection, the field grows denser.

But the lines do not fuse.

The dark junction of Rox's tattoo appears—not on her body now, but as a
structural relation within the field.

Two trajectories.

One intersection.

An interior made possible by their meeting.

The field attempts to hold the intersection permanently.

Immediately, the structure begins to fail.

The lines tighten.

The membrane loses permeability.

What had been an encounter becomes a fixed arrangement.

The field releases its hold.


                                        272

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 273 / 286>

The structure moves again.

And the relation survives.

Something becomes available that was not available before.

Sustain is not the prevention of change. Sustain is the continuing capacity
to undergo change without abolishing the distinctions that make relation
possible.

The three encounter-images return briefly.

Eleanor does not become Mara.

Mara does not become Rox.

Rox does not become the culmination of either.

And Him does not become the owner of their meanings simply because his
experience has brought them together.

The field can hold their relations.

It cannot turn them into one person's destiny.




THE UNEXPECTED RELATION
The Read has been following what an event leaves behind.

The Collision Field has been investigating what allows different temporal
histories to participate in a continuing relation.

Now they encounter each other.

The Read asks:

What remains when an event has passed?

The Collision Field asks:

What if the event has not finished becoming consequential?

A deposit ordinarily appears to follow an event.

                                        273

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 274 / 286>

First the occurrence.

Then the remainder.

But the field reveals a different possibility.

The deposit may participate in later encounters that change its meaning
without changing the fact or time of its origin.

A mark made at nineteen can be read differently years later.

A drawing made in 1978 can become newly consequential tonight.

A kiss can change the future significance of a word that existed centuries
before either person was born.

The timestamps remain.

The relations change.

And a further distinction emerges.

An event ends in chronological time without necessarily exhausting its
capacity to participate in meaning.

The field grows quiet.

Not motionless.

Quiet in the way a flame can become steady while continuing to consume,
exchange, and transform.

The apparent center dissolves.

No image of Him remains to witness the result.

No character announces it.

The page alone carries the deposit.

RETURN — WITHOUT THE WITNESS
The Read returns:

DEPOSIT IS WHAT AN EVENT LEAVES AVAILABLE TO A FUTURE IT DOES
NOT OWN.

The Collision Field returns:

SUSTAIN IS THE CONTINUING RELATION THAT ALLOWS DIFFERENT
TIMES TO PARTICIPATE WITHOUT LOSING THEIR DISTINCT HISTORIES.

Their intersection produces something neither inquiry contained alone.

A deposit is not merely a remainder waiting to be interpreted.

It can become an active condition in a later encounter.

And sustain is not merely the capacity to preserve what has arrived.

It is the capacity to keep that arrival available for further transformation.

The two operations converge.

A DEPOSIT CAN ENTER A SUSTAIN WITHOUT SURRENDERING ITS
TIMESTAMP.

A SUSTAIN CAN CHANGE THE MEANING OF A DEPOSIT WITHOUT
CHANGING WHEN IT WAS MADE.

The page holds the finding.

But there is one more consequence.

The Guardian cannot preserve only the present boundary.

It must also protect the integrity of what enters from the past and the openness
of what may emerge into the future.

The Invisible Architect cannot construct only the conditions for arrival.

It must construct conditions under which an arrival may become
consequential without becoming captive to the structure that received it.


                                       275

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 276 / 286>

And the Return cannot be complete merely because a coherent meaning has
emerged.

It must leave the relation capable of being revised again.

The page writes:

THE SUSTAIN HOLDS ONLY WHILE THE FUTURE REMAINS UNOWNED.




THE ROOM
The lamp is still burning.

Rox is beside Him.

Her leg rests across his.

The rug has not moved.

The brass instrument remains on the table.

No evidence in the room establishes that Rox experienced the images, that
Mara or Eleanor shared the interval, or that any event outside Him's
experience occurred.

The three histories remain distinct.

His awareness returns gradually.

He hears Rox breathe.

He feels the weight of her hand.

The familiar world has not been replaced.

Something has been deposited in the account of his encounter.

He does not yet know what it will allow him to ask.

And Rox, undisturbed, sleeps beside him.

On the page that no one in the room can read, the final inscription appears:

DEPOSIT ⇄ SUSTAIN

What continues must leave room for what has not yet arrived.

The page does not close the threshold.

It leaves it permeable.


THE READ — DEPOSIT
The word enters without a speaker.

DEPOSIT.

At first, it appears to mean something placed somewhere.

Something laid down.

Something entrusted to a surface, a vessel, a keeper.

The Read follows the word backward.

Latin deponere.

De- — down, away.

Ponere — to place.

To lay down.

To put aside.

To entrust.

To leave something in a place where it may remain after the act of placing has
ended.

But the word has never belonged to one domain.

A river deposits sediment.

A witness gives a deposition.

A person deposits money with an institution.

A living organism deposits material that becomes part of its structure.
                                      277

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 278 / 286>

A memory leaves a trace.

Each use contains a different relation between an event and what remains.

And the Read encounters a distinction.

The deposit is not the event that produced it.

Sediment is not the river.

Testimony is not the witnessed occurrence.

A memory is not the moment remembered.

Yet each can alter what becomes possible afterward.

The riverbed changes.

The testimony enters a history of knowing.

The remembered encounter changes what someone may recognize next.

The Read turns toward the three scenes.

Rox on the rug.

Mara beneath the blanket.

Eleanor at the dinner table.

Each encounter has left something.

But no deposit contains the whole person from whom it arose.

And none authorizes the reader to claim that person's future.

The word begins to change.

A deposit is not simply something preserved.

It is something that can become consequential after the event that produced it
is no longer present.

Then the Read encounters its first resistance.

A deposit can be misread.


                                      278

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 279 / 286>

A witness can remember incorrectly.

Sediment can be moved.

A record can be altered.

The deposit carries a history, but it does not automatically tell the truth about
that history.

So the Read must preserve two questions:

WHAT WAS LEFT?

WHAT CAN THE REMAINDER WARRANT?

And a third emerges.

WHAT CAN BEGIN BECAUSE SOMETHING WAS LEFT?

The Read does not resolve them.

It passes them into the Collision Field.




THE COLLISION FIELD — SUSTAIN / PERMEABLE
TIME

GRAVITY
The field receives the deposit.

Not as an object.

As a relation between an event and its remainder.

It receives sustain.

Not as an arrested instant.

As continuing activity through which different histories can remain available
together.

And it receives permeable time.

                                           279

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 280 / 286>

Not as the abolition of chronology.

As the possibility that consequences from different times can participate in one
present without surrendering their origins.

The three encounter-images remain.

Rox.

Mara.

Eleanor.

Each carries a different history.

Each resists becoming the explanation of the others.

The field begins pulling at their representations.

The dinner table separates from Eleanor.

The blanket separates from Mara.

The rug separates from Rox.

The images no longer provide stable locations.

But their differences do not disappear.

The field encounters its first contradiction.

To sustain the relation, it must allow movement.

To preserve the participants, it must maintain distinction.

A perfectly fixed relation cannot receive anything new.

A relation without boundaries cannot retain anything distinct.

The gravity intensifies.

And what begins to fail is not the boundary.

It is the assumption that a boundary must be stationary to remain real.
