## Exact proposed CF-06 image prompt (draft)

Create a monumental, nearly square abstract artwork as one continuous physical event in hand-worked lime plaster, translucent casein washes, powdered graphite, and thin burnished wax. An expansive, quiet, warm alabaster field occupies most of the composition, with small passages of cool mineral grey and deep iron-oxide red held inside the surface rather than lit by a synthetic glow.

Across its lower middle, an oblique, shallow relief disturbance appears at first glance to be one long motion pressing through the plaster. From farther away, its softly gathered ridges seem to belong to one continuous occurrence. On closer examination, two separated pressure histories become visible: one trough has compressed and displaced the plaster into a crisp lip, while the nearby, very similar impression has nearly the same contour but lacks a consistent direction of displaced material. Some fine graphite deposits seem to continue from one to the other, then stop before contact. Neither depression explains the origin of the other. The work is not a diptych, comparison chart, or before-and-after sequence: both marks occupy one uneven, continuous field.

Within a short rising fold of this material, incorporate the partial contour of a clearly adult human lower leg—an indistinct calf and bent knee, neither a freestanding limb nor a posed person. The calf's soft volume becomes the mineral ridge itself; the knee's tension gently raises one part of the plaster without striking or contacting the second impression. Flesh emerges only through warm skin undertones, delicate living curvature, and subtle porous texture before disappearing into casein, mineral dust, and wax. The bodily motion is held, withheld, and integrated with the surface, not acting upon a symbolic object. No full character, isolated hand, or erotic emphasis. No explicit anatomy.

The composition's upper half opens into profound, nearly untouched vacuous space. This is a charged, uncompleted interval: it carries the suspension of a motion that may not have happened, rather than serving as an ordinary background. Let the relief fade unevenly before reaching that open region. A low grazing light from outside the frame reveals surface height and fragile minute cracks without spotlighting a center. Far view: one graceful displacement. Middle view: an unstable relation between two apparent marks and one emergent bodily contour. Near view: the edges of pigment, ridges, abrasions, and deposits do not give a conclusive accounting of the forces that made them.

This artwork must feel original, luminous, spacious, materially exact, and emotionally restrained. Painterly labor and revision must be legible in the surface. No wood, stacked pages, printing press, hammer, book illustration, collage scraps, parchment, architectural portals, fantasy landscape, luminous filaments, circuitry, cyan-and-gold sky, or opposing edge-placed body and hand. No letters, words, typography, numerals, symbols, captions, UI elements, decorative diagrams, or text-like marks of any kind.

## Why this differs morphologically (not exegesis)

- One continuous shallow mineral-relief field, rather than a scene, portal, book, split display or assembled surreal set.
- A lower-middle, non-axial pressure topology rather than right-hand/left-torso silhouette.
- Far/middle/near perception of event versus indeterminate material history; neither proximity nor resemblance proves two originating acts.
- Partial knee/calf becomes the raised geological material itself, rather than a person next to a symbolic object.
- Upper-field emptiness is a necessary withheld interval, not blank atmosphere pasted behind an icon.
- No selected historical material-substitution pair; the physical contrast is between consequences of pressure within the *same* visual field.
