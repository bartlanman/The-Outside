# Collision Field 04 — Outside Sources

## Parody
Poetry Foundation — Glossary of Poetic Terms: Parody
https://www.poetryfoundation.org/education/glossary/parody

Supports:
- parody as imitation of a writer, work, or style;
- imitation used for comic or satirical effect.

Project inference:
- parody preserves enough recognizable structure for distortion to register;
- violation may function as recognition.

---

## Memes
Limor Shifman, *Memes in Digital Culture*, MIT Press:
https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262525435/memes-in-digital-culture/

Supports:
- memes as cultural units circulating through imitation and transformation;
- internet memes as groups of related digital items created with awareness of one another and circulated / transformed by many users.

Project inference:
- survival through repeated alteration;
- recognizability persisting despite mutation.

---

## Improvisation
The Second City — improv training materials / classes:
https://www.secondcity.com/

Supports broadly:
- improvisational performance built through ensemble relation;
- acceptance and addition as a foundational practice in many improv traditions.

Important:
"Yes, and" is not a universal metaphysical law. Use it here only as one practical tradition of collaborative improvisation.

Project inference:
- freedom entering through obligation to the existing offer;
- participation without sovereignty.

## Carnival / Bakhtin
Mikhail Bakhtin, *Rabelais and His World*.

Supporting overview:
https://www.britannica.com/biography/Mikhail-Bakhtin

Use for:
- carnival;
- inversion;
- grotesque realism;
- laughter directed at official seriousness and hierarchy.

Project inference:
- the rule becoming visible through inversion;
- the fool requiring the crown.

## Ritual Clowning / Sacred Clowns

This domain varies radically across specific traditions.

Any future use must be sourced tradition-by-tradition and must not flatten Indigenous, ritual, ceremonial, or religious clowning into one universal category.

For this collision field, ritual clowning remains a cautionary outside draw rather than a generalized source claim.

Do not use sacred-clown examples merely as metaphor without cultural context and provenance.

---

# Source Handling Rule

These materials establish outside practices on their own terms.

They do **not** establish:
- VIOLATION AS RECOGNITION as a universal law;
- SURVIVING MISUSE as a criterion of artistic life;
- affectionate deformation as ethically harmless;
- "WHAT DOES THE JOKE KEEP?" as a scholarly conclusion.

Those are project-level collisions and poetic inferences.

Preserve the difference.
