# I. ISE JINGU — THE THING SURVIVES BY BEING REPLACED

## On its own terms

At Ise Jingu, the Shikinen Sengu periodically renews shrine buildings and transfers the sacred symbol to a newly constructed divine palace.

The physical structure is rebuilt.

The continuity is not located solely in the persistence of the same material object.

What persists includes:
- pattern;
- rite;
- craft;
- transmission;
- capacity to remake.

## What enters our field

Ordinary preservation imagines:

**ORIGINAL
→ PROTECT
→ PRESERVE**

Ise introduces:

**FORM
→ REMAKE
→ TRANSMIT
→ REMAKE
→ TRANSMIT**

The physical instance can disappear while continuity remains.

A crucial distinction appears:

> **THE AFTERLIFE OF AN EVENT CAN BE A CAPACITY.**

Not merely a record of what happened.

A capacity to make again.

Do not promote yet.

# II. GUSTAV METZGER — THE THING CAN BECOME ITSELF BY DISAPPEARING

## On its own terms

Metzger's auto-destructive work made destruction part of artistic realization rather than simply an external failure imposed on the artwork.

The work could change over time.

Disappearance could belong to the form.

## Collision with Ise

Ise:

> destroy / replace the instance so a continuity can remain.

Metzger:

> permit destruction because destruction belongs to the work's realization.

These are not identical.

Together they disturb a hidden assumption:

> a masterwork survives because its object survives.

Possible proliferation:

> **A WORK MAY HAVE A DEATH CONDITION.**

Not failure.

Completion.

This intensifies the existing gravity of IRREVERSIBILITY:

> **SOME THINGS MAY NEED TO BECOME IRRECOVERABLE IN ORDER FOR THE WORK TO HAVE OCCURRED.**

Keep in the field.

# III. ECOLOGICAL SUCCESSION — SOMETHING CAN CREATE THE CONDITIONS THAT REPLACE IT

## On its own terms

In ecological succession, organisms enter a field and alter its conditions.

Those altered conditions can make later communities possible.

The community that changes the field need not remain the dominant community.

A form can help produce the conditions of its own replacement.

## What enters our field

A possible motion:

**A inhabits FIELD
→ A alters FIELD
→ altered FIELD makes B possible
→ B replaces or transforms A**

This is not ordinary branching.

It is not merely a score producing one of several anticipated outcomes.

The process itself can create new conditions of possibility.

Possible phrase:

> **GENERATIVE OBSOLESCENCE**

or simply:

> **SUCCESSION**

The important question is:

> **CAN THE WORK PRODUCE CONDITIONS THAT MAKE A FORM POSSIBLE WHICH THE ORIGINAL WORK COULD NOT HAVE CONTAINED?**

This extends DEVELOPMENTAL EMERGENCE.

The future form may not exist in compressed form at the beginning.

# IV. LOOSE-PARTS PLAY — POSSIBILITY CAN LIVE IN THE UNASSIGNED

## On its own terms

Loose-parts play provides moveable, open-ended materials without prescribing one fixed use.

The material has affordances.

The activity is not fully assigned in advance.

A board may become:
- bridge;
- wall;
- roof;
- ramp;
- counter;
- boundary;
- lever;
- prop;
- nothing.

The expressive possibility exists between:

**THING × SITUATION × IMAGINATION × OTHER THINGS**

## What enters our field

Possible phrase:

> **UNASSIGNED CAPACITY**

The maker need not author the eventual symbolic function.

The maker may place something into the world that is rich enough to acquire functions through relation.

This matters for Mythos.

Perhaps Mythos does not always arrive as a completed symbolic system.

Perhaps something becomes symbolically charged downstream because participants repeatedly encounter, use, remember, distort, joke about, ritualize, fear, or love it.

So:

> **DO NOT NECESSARILY WRITE THE SYMBOL.  
> MAKE SOMETHING CAPABLE OF BECOMING ONE.**

# VI. FIRST GRAVITY — CAPACITY RATHER THAN OBJECT

Something has started pulling.

Not yet doctrine:

> **THE WORK MAY SURVIVE AS A CAPACITY RATHER THAN AS AN OBJECT.**

A second formulation:

> **WHAT REMAINS AFTER AN EVENT MAY NOT BE A RECORD OF THE EVENT.  
> IT MAY BE A CHANGED ABILITY TO PRODUCE THE NEXT EVENT.**

This changes TRACE.

The current motion had been approximated as:

**SCORE / FIELD
→ RELATION
→ EMERGENT GRAMMAR
→ EVENT
→ TRACE**

Collision Field 03 suggests that EVENT may produce several different consequences:

**EVENT
→ TRACE**

something records what occurred;

**EVENT
→ FIELD CHANGE**

the conditions are different afterward;

**EVENT
→ CAPACITY CHANGE**

someone or something can now do something it could not do before.

These can diverge.

A person can forget the event and retain the capacity.

A culture can lose an artifact and retain the practice.

A place can lose readable evidence of an event while its ecology remains altered.

A system can preserve enormous traces while acquiring no meaningful new capacity.

# VIII. CAGE-BREAK — WHAT MUST NOT CHANGE?

The strongest inverse question:

> **WHAT IS SO IMPORTANT THAT IT MUST NOT BE ALLOWED TO CHANGE, DEVELOP, REGENERATE, RECOMPOSE, OR BE REINTERPRETED INTO SOMETHING ELSE?**

The answer is not simply:

> some things must remain static.

A more interesting possibility appears:

> **SOME THINGS MUST REMAIN INVARIANT SO THAT EVERYTHING ELSE IS FREE TO CHANGE.**

# IX. VEDIC CHANTING — A LIVING CAPACITY DEVOTED TO INVARIANCE

## On its own terms

The Vedic chanting tradition uses highly developed oral recitation techniques to preserve transmitted texts and their sound with exceptional fidelity.

This is living transmission:
- teacher;
- student;
- breath;
- body;
- memory;
- performance.

But the increase in human skill does not necessarily increase variation.

The capacity is cultivated partly in order to constrain variation.

## What enters our field

CAPACITY splits.

### GENERATIVE CAPACITY

Ability to make something previously impossible.

### FIDELITY CAPACITY

Ability to carry something through change without losing it.

These are not opposites.

They are different offices of capacity.

# X. THE ARCHIVE — DO NOT CORRECT THE PAST

## On its own terms

The National Archives distinguishes archival authenticity from the informational accuracy of a record.

An archival record may contain incorrect information and still need to be preserved authentically as the record that was actually created.

This creates a severe distinction:

**TRUTH ABOUT THE EVENT**

is not identical to

**TRUTH ABOUT THE RECORD THAT WAS MADE.**

If a historical witness wrote the wrong day, correcting the document could destroy evidence of what that witness actually recorded.

## What enters our field

This introduces:

# CUSTODY

Not ownership.

Not authorship.

Not simple preservation.

Something can be entrusted to a carrier who is permitted to transmit or care for it but not permitted to make it into whatever they wish.

Possible law:

> **RETURN MAY ALTER INTERPRETATION WITHOUT ALTERING THE WITNESS.**

The archive allows endless re-reading.

It does not grant sovereignty over the deposited record.

---

# XI. CHAIN OF CUSTODY — CONTINUITY CAN INCLUDE PROVENANCE

Custodial history matters because changes in ownership, custody, or transmission can affect interpretation, authenticity, and integrity.

This expands continuity.

A thing may not only carry content.

It may carry the history of how it arrived.

So the masterwork question acquires another dimension:

> Can provenance itself possess standing?

A future realization may need to know not only **what** something is, but **how it got here**.

---

# XII. THE HASH — ONE BIT MATTERS

Cryptographic hashing introduces an extreme relation to invariance.

A small change in input produces a very different hash output.

Hashing can therefore be used to test exact digital identity / integrity rather than semantic similarity.

Artistically, this supplies a useful counterforce to generative culture.

Not:

> basically the same.

But:

> this exact deposit has changed, or it has not.

Possible proliferation:

A work contains something that cannot be regenerated.

There is one deposit.

Copies may circulate.

Interpretations may proliferate.

AI may discuss it.

Participants may mythologize it.

But the work can still retain:

> **THIS IS WHAT ACTUALLY ENTERED THE RECORD.**

A world may require things that not only refuse to answer.

It may require things that refuse to change.

# XIII. RESTORATION — CHANGE WITHOUT COUNTERFEITING HISTORY

The Venice Charter permits conservation and restoration while insisting that intervention not falsify artistic or historic evidence.

This produces another relation to change:

not:

**NEVER TOUCH ANYTHING**

and not:

**REMAKE IT AS IF NOTHING HAPPENED**

but something closer to:

**ORIGINAL
→ CHANGE
→ ORIGINAL + LEGIBLE CHANGE
→ CHANGE
→ ORIGINAL + HISTORY OF CHANGE**

Possible law:

> **A LIVING WORK DOES NOT HAVE TO HIDE ITS SCARS IN ORDER TO REMAIN COHERENT.**

Sometimes hiding the scar destroys truth.

# XIV. SECOND GRAVITY — DIFFERENTIAL MUTABILITY

The cage-break does not kill CAPACITY.

It differentiates it.

A new gravity appears:

# DIFFERENTIAL MUTABILITY

Different things inside a work may possess different rights, capacities, or obligations regarding change.

Possible standings:

## FIXED
Must remain exactly what entered the work.

## CUSTODIAL
Its carrier may change, but something must be transmitted faithfully.

## INTERPRETABLE
The deposit remains fixed while meaning around it changes.

## RESTORABLE
It may be repaired, but intervention must not counterfeit its history.

## ADAPTIVE
It changes in response to circumstance.

## GENERATIVE
It can produce novel realizations.

## DEVELOPMENTAL
It can acquire capacities not possessed initially.

## EPHEMERAL
It exists only once.

## DESTRUCTIVE
Its disappearance belongs to realization.

## SUCCESSIONAL
It changes the field and may then be legitimately replaced.

Do not canonicalize this taxonomy.

It is a way of noticing the collision.

> **A WORK MAY DISTRIBUTE ITS CONTINUITY ACROSS OBJECTS, CAPACITIES, PRACTICES, WITNESSES, AND TRANSFORMATIONS — EACH WITH A DIFFERENT RELATION TO CHANGE.**

Some things develop.

Some things disappear.

> **SOME THINGS HAVE TO REMAIN WHERE THEY WERE SO THAT WE CAN TELL THAT WE HAVE MOVED.**

Difference requires something against which difference can appear.
