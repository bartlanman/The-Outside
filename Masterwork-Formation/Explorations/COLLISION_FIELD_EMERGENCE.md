Do not use an enormous open landscape. Do not use a horizon. Do not use portals, bridges, towers, glowing webs, filament networks, mystical architecture, sacred geometry, or a hand manipulating the field.

Construct the image as a dense but breathable cross-section of soft and hardened materials: thin mineral sediment, wet plaster, compressed pulp, translucent wax, fine granular deposits, dried skin-like films, and shallow flows that have accumulated sequentially through time.

Near the interior of the composition—not anchored to an outer edge—include one anatomically incomplete zone of living human flesh: a partial lateral rib-and-waist curvature with only a suggestion of adjoining back, no head, no face, no full torso, no limbs, and no anatomy sufficient to reconstruct a complete person. The important event occurs after that contact.
