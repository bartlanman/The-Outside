# 1. Public adoption and ordinary exposure

## Pew Research Center — Americans and AI 2026

Published June 17, 2026.

Findings include:
- 49% of U.S. adults report using AI chatbots;
- 24% report daily chatbot use;
- 36% say they interact with AI at least several times a day;
- search and work are leading uses;
- 10% report chatbot use for emotional support;
- 4% report companionship use.

Source:
https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/

Status:
DOCUMENTED SURVEY.

# 2. AI-authored web content

## Pew Research Center — How Much of the Internet Is Written With AI?

Published August 20, 2026.

Pew sampled 490,000 English-language webpages from Common Crawl.

It reports substantial and rising signs of AI authorship, including significant AI authorship in roughly one in ten pages in the July 2026 snapshot and much higher prevalence among pages published after ChatGPT's release.

Source:
https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai/

Status:
DOCUMENTED EMPIRICAL STUDY.

# 3. Synthetic people do not reproduce actual public opinion

## Pew Research Center — Can AI Stand In for Human Survey-Takers? Not Really

Published September 30, 2026.

Findings include:
- synthetic survey estimates consistently differed from human survey results;
- average absolute error across three waves was about 12.4 percentage points;
- some human-selected answer categories were entirely absent in synthetic samples;
- synthetic respondents differed substantially from humans in uncertainty and factual-error behavior;
- different model choices produced systematically different representations of the same population.

Sources:
https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/can-ai-stand-in-for-human-survey-takers-not-really/
https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/how-well-synthetic-samples-replicate-public-opinion/
https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/how-synthetic-polling-results-change-based-on-the-ai-model/

Status:
DOCUMENTED EMPIRICAL STUDY.

# 5. Linguistic diversity / homogenization

## Nature Human Behaviour — 2026

"The shrinking landscape of linguistic diversity in the age of large language models."

Across seven datasets and more than 880,000 texts, the study reports that LLM writing assistance is linked to reduced linguistic diversity, with core content often retained while variance in writing style and complexity is compressed.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02550-0

# 7. Cultural representation and compression

## ACL / EMNLP / NAACL research

Relevant findings include:
- LLMs encode and prioritize different value patterns across languages and cultures;
- cultural adaptation based on insufficient representations can homogenize norms;
- models can fail to reproduce the full spectrum of cross-cultural moral variation;
- prompt language and cultural framing affect outputs without eliminating systematic cultural bias.

Sources:
https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.274/
https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.912/
https://aclanthology.org/2025.luhme-1.3/
https://aclanthology.org/2026.cl-2.2/

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED CONFERENCE FINDINGS.
