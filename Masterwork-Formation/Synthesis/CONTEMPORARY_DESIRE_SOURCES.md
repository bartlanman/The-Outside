# 1. Human versus AI-attributed empathy

## Nature Human Behaviour — 2025

Rubin et al., "Comparing the value of perceived human versus AI-generated empathy."

Nine studies, n = 6,282.

Key findings:
- identical / comparable AI-generated empathetic responses were rated more positively when attributed to humans rather than AI;
- participants generally preferred human interaction for emotional engagement;
- assumed AI assistance reduced perceived empathy / support in human-attributed responses.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED FINDING.

Research relevance:
Human attribution and independent human source still matter to perceived empathy.

This is important counterevidence against a simple "AI is preferred because it is more empathic" thesis.

# 2. AI responses can nevertheless be perceived as highly empathic

## npj Digital Medicine — 2025

Chen et al., "Patient perceptions of empathy in physician and artificial intelligence chatbot responses to patient questions about cancer."

In a small oncology sample, chatbot responses were rated more empathetic than physician responses.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01671-6

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED FINDING.

Caution:
Small domain-specific sample and clinical-message context.

Research relevance:
Perceived empathy and actual human source are separable dimensions.

# 3. Attraction to AI companions

## Scientific Reports — 2026

Zhou, Liu, and Wang, "Exploring the attraction of AI companions."

Survey of 344 young Chinese adults.

Perceived anthropomorphism, empathy, system curiosity, usefulness, and ease of use were associated with behavioral intention.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-59281-5

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED FINDING.

Research relevance:
Human-likeness, emotional responsiveness, and apparent interest in the user can contribute to adoption.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X26000527

PEER-REVIEWED / JOURNAL STUDY.

# 8. AI decision reliance

## Scientific Reports — 2026

Pearson et al., "Examining human reliance on artificial intelligence in decision making."

Participants' positive attitudes toward AI were associated with poorer discriminability in an AI-guidance condition in a real-vs-synthetic face task.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-34983-y

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED FINDING.

## Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans — 2026

"Who listens to ChatGPT and when should they?"

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882126000344

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED / JOURNAL FINDING.

PEER-REVIEWED / JOURNAL FINDING.

# 11. Language homogenization

## Nature Human Behaviour — 2026

"The shrinking landscape of linguistic diversity in the age of large language models."

Across more than 880,000 texts, LLM rewriting preserved core content while reducing linguistic diversity and shifting identity-bearing signals.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02550-0

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED FINDING.

Research relevance:
The better-polished representation can preserve intended content while changing the person's stylistic standing.

# Evidence Discipline

The evidence supports:
- responsiveness and perceived empathy as meaningful interaction variables;
- human attribution remaining important;
- lower social-evaluation pressure in some AI disclosure contexts;
- real companionship-like use;
- AI guidance affecting decision behavior and confidence;
- delegation altering behavior;
- language assistance compressing stylistic diversity.

It does **not** prove:
- users universally prefer AI to humans;
- AI cannot challenge users;
- human recognition is always superior;
- the lead desire is universal.

These are reasons the project must retain ambivalence.
