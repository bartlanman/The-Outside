# Story-World Seeds

## Status

Pre-plot story-world seeds for the Relational Return.

These are **not plots**.

They do not specify:
- twists;
- endings;
- full character arcs;
- final interpretations.

Each seed tests whether a world can naturally carry:

**CARE -> CONTACT -> CHANGED STANDING -> CHANGED OBLIGATION -> RETURN**

with a stable fixed core and a live seam.

---

# Seed 1 — The House With a Working History

## What is being cared for

An old house that has remained in use for generations.

Not a museum.

A living place:
- repaired;
- inhabited;
- maintained;
- altered carefully over time.

## Current custodian

A person who inherited responsibility for the house and has spent years keeping it structurally sound without turning it into a frozen historical object.

## Why the first account is warranted

The custodian's care is visibly competent.

They:
- know the building;
- preserve useful old work;
- replace what fails;
- resist fashionable restoration;
- understand that a living house must keep changing.

The first reading can sincerely establish:

> THIS PERSON IS A GOOD STEWARD.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible classes:
- original deed;
- public land record;
- building survey;
- archaeological trace;
- prior resident archive;
- boundary record;
- material feature uncovered during repair.

The handoff should not reveal merely:
"the house has a secret."

It should reveal that another relation to the house has standing.

## What obligation might change

The question could shift from:

HOW DO I PRESERVE THIS HOUSE?

to:

WHAT AM I ENTITLED TO PRESERVE, ALTER, KEEP, OPEN, RETURN, OR SHARE?

## Returnable anchors

1. A repaired doorway.
2. A repeated phrase about "keeping the house standing."
3. A patch of floor / wall whose repair appears repeatedly.

## Possible changed standing

The repaired doorway might first read as:
CARE.

Later it may read as:
A DECISION ABOUT WHO OR WHAT WAS ALLOWED TO REMAIN LEGIBLE.

## Remainder

No handoff could fully settle:
- what preservation owes to current life;
- what belongs to the dead;
- what belongs to the living;
- whether stewardship requires keeping, changing, or relinquishing.

## Risk

Can become:
SECRET HOUSE HISTORY.

Avoid puzzle capture.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

# Seed 2 — The Archive of One Life

## What is being cared for

A private archive left by a person whose work mattered locally but never became famous.

Materials might include:
- notebooks;
- letters;
- photographs;
- recordings;
- receipts;
- drafts;
- tools;
- unfinished work.

## Current custodian

A careful archivist, relative, apprentice, or local institution trying to preserve the person's legacy without inventing importance.

## Why the first account is warranted

The custodian is disciplined.

They:
- catalog;
- date;
- preserve;
- separate evidence from inference;
- resist romanticizing gaps.

The first reading establishes:

> THIS PERSON IS KEEPING THE RECORD HONEST.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- a separately held letter;
- another archive's accession;
- public record;
- an unconnected recipient's material;
- a dated photograph;
- an institutional file;
- an independently preserved copy of a disputed work.

## What obligation might change

The problem could move from:

WHAT SHOULD BE PRESERVED?

to:

WHO HAS THE RIGHT TO DEFINE WHAT THIS LIFE MEANS?

or:

WHAT SHOULD REMAIN PRIVATE EVEN IF IT CHANGES THE PUBLIC RECORD?

## Returnable anchors

1. A labeled archival box.
2. A photograph repeatedly described but not fully interpreted.
3. A phrase in the custodian's catalog notes.

## Possible changed standing

The label first reads as:
ACCURATE DESCRIPTION.

After contact:
AN ACT OF FRAMING.

## Remainder

The archive will never equal the person.

Preservation remains:
SELECTION.

## Risk

Can become:
FOUND-DOCUMENT MYSTERY.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

# Seed 3 — The Repair Shop

## What is being cared for

A small workshop where damaged objects are repaired rather than replaced.

The objects could be:
- instruments;
- clocks;
- furniture;
- tools;
- machines;
- books.

## Current custodian

A highly skilled repairer who inherited the shop's methods and believes a good repair preserves use without pretending damage never happened.

## Why the first account is warranted

The work itself demonstrates the ethic.

The repairer:
- leaves honest seams;
- uses compatible materials;
- preserves wear;
- refuses cosmetic perfection when it destroys history.

The first reading can establish:

> REPAIR MEANS KEEPING SOMETHING IN RELATION WITH ITS OWN HISTORY.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- maker's original specification;
- owner record;
- prior repair;
- patent / manual;
- provenance document;
- material analysis;
- successor claim.

## What obligation might change

The repairer may discover that:
the thing they are preserving as one object
has standing as:
- another maker's alteration;
- another owner's adaptation;
- contested property;
- composite work.

Then:

WHAT COUNTS AS THE OBJECT?

becomes unstable.

## Returnable anchors

1. A visible seam.
2. A drawer of removed parts.
3. The phrase "don't erase the break."

## Possible changed standing

Removed parts first read as:
WASTE / FAILED MATERIAL.

Later:
EVIDENCE / PRIOR AUTHORSHIP.

## Remainder

No final repair can perfectly separate:
ORIGINAL
from
LATER LIFE.

## Risk

Can become overly symbolic if every object is made to "mean" the thesis.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

# Seed 4 — The Community Hall

## What is being cared for

A building used for:
- meetings;
- celebrations;
- meals;
- music;
- funerals;
- civic gatherings.

Its importance comes from use rather than architectural prestige.

## Current custodian

A small board, manager, caretaker, or family that has kept the building open through financial and physical difficulty.

## Why the first account is warranted

Their care has preserved a real communal function.

They have made compromises:
- repairs;
- rentals;
- schedule decisions;
- rules;
- fundraising.

The first reading establishes:

> WITHOUT THIS CUSTODY, THE PLACE WOULD BE GONE.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- old charter;
- deed restriction;
- municipal record;
- another group's historic claim;
- accessibility requirement;
- neighborhood testimony;
- land-use decision.

## What obligation might change

The question could shift from:

HOW DO WE KEEP THE HALL OPEN?

to:

WHO MUST THE HALL REMAIN OPEN FOR?

or:

DOES SAVING THE BUILDING REQUIRE CHANGING WHO CONTROLS IT?

## Returnable anchors

1. A key ring.
2. A booking calendar.
3. A long table used for many different events.

## Possible changed standing

The key ring first reads as:
RESPONSIBILITY.

Later:
CONTROL.

## Remainder

No governance arrangement can perfectly reconcile:
- preservation;
- access;
- memory;
- solvency;
- ownership.

## Risk

Can become issue fiction or local-politics allegory.

## Strength

**HIGH.**

# Seed 5 — The Orchard

## What is being cared for

An old orchard where trees have been grafted, replanted, and maintained across generations.

The orchard is productive, not ornamental.

## Current custodian

A grower who believes stewardship means keeping the orchard alive rather than preserving every tree exactly as inherited.

## Why the first account is warranted

The grower understands:
- pruning;
- grafting;
- disease;
- soil;
- weather;
- market pressure.

They make hard choices necessary for continued life.

The first reading establishes:

> CUTTING CAN BE A FORM OF CARE.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- genetic analysis;
- old nursery records;
- water rights;
- ecological survey;
- neighboring land history;
- disease event;
- climate condition;
- successor claim.

## What obligation might change

A discovery could change what counts as:
- native;
- inherited;
- valuable;
- viable;
- owned.

The grower may need to reconsider whether preserving "the orchard" means preserving:
- species;
- lineage;
- yield;
- landscape;
- community use;
- adaptation.

## Returnable anchors

1. A graft scar.
2. A pruning knife.
3. A particular row crossed repeatedly.

## Possible changed standing

The graft scar first reads as:
SKILLFUL CONTINUITY.

Later:
EVIDENCE THAT CONTINUITY WAS ALWAYS COMPOSITE.

## Remainder

There may be no pure original orchard to return to.

## Risk

Can become ecological parable.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

# Seed 6 — The Unfinished Score

## What is being cared for

An unfinished musical composition left by a deceased composer or bandleader.

Enough survives to perform parts of it.

Not enough survives to know one final intended form.

## Current custodian

A former student, ensemble leader, archivist, or family-appointed steward preparing the work for performance.

## Why the first account is warranted

The custodian knows:
- the composer's habits;
- prior drafts;
- notation;
- performance practice;
- what kinds of completion would be stylistically plausible.

The first reading establishes:

> SOMEONE HAS TO MAKE RESPONSIBLE DECISIONS OR THE WORK CANNOT BE HEARD.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- newly discovered rehearsal tape;
- performer annotations;
- another manuscript;
- correspondence;
- rights-holder decision;
- earlier rejected passage.

## What obligation might change

The question becomes:

IS STEWARDSHIP COMPLETION?

or:

DOES PRESERVING THE WORK REQUIRE LEAVING SOME PART UNFINISHED?

## Returnable anchors

1. A repeated unresolved musical phrase.
2. A page with a blank measure.
3. A conductor's penciled cue.

## Possible changed standing

The blank measure first reads as:
MISSING CONTENT.

Later:
A BOUNDARY THE STEWARD MAY NOT BE ENTITLED TO CROSS.

## Remainder

No source may establish the final intended version.

## Risk

Can become authorship thesis too directly.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

# Seed 7 — The Family Business Ledger

## What is being cared for

A small multi-generation business kept alive through difficult years.

The core is not "business drama."

It is:
- work;
- accounts;
- routines;
- equipment;
- customer relationships;
- inherited obligations.

## Current custodian

The person now running the business.

They believe stewardship means:
- paying people;
- keeping the place open;
- preserving useful traditions;
- changing whatever must change to survive.

## Why the first account is warranted

The business genuinely survives because of their decisions.

The first reading establishes:

> THIS PERSON IS CARRYING SOMETHING OTHER PEOPLE COULD NOT OR WOULD NOT CARRY.

## Independent standing that could enter

Possible:
- old partnership agreement;
- forgotten debt instrument;
- employee record;
- property restriction;
- supplier archive;
- prior owner's letter;
- public filing;
- historical customer account.

## What obligation might change

The central question can shift from:

HOW DO I SAVE THIS BUSINESS?

to:

WHAT EXACTLY AM I SAVING, AND FOR WHOM?

A financially correct act may become relationally insufficient.

## Returnable anchors

1. The ledger.
2. A machine repaired repeatedly.
3. A closing-time routine.

## Possible changed standing

The ledger first reads as:
SURVIVAL / RESPONSIBILITY.

Later:
A RECORD OF WHO ABSORBED THE COST OF SURVIVAL.

## Remainder

There may be no clean way to preserve:
- institution;
- jobs;
- inheritance;
- fairness;
- identity

all at once.

## Risk

Can become economics lecture or family-secret plot.

## Strength

**VERY HIGH.**

HOW DO WE KEEP PEOPLE CROSSING?

WHOSE CROSSING IS THIS?

WHEN DOES MAINTAINING ACCESS BECOME MAINTAINING A HARMFUL RELATION?

## Risk

## Strength

**HIGH.**

# Strongest Seeds After First Pass

Four seeds currently carry the architecture especially well without requiring twist mechanics:

## A. The Repair Shop

Why:
- repair already embodies revision without erasure;
- material trace is natural;
- "removed parts" can retain standing;
- care and intervention are inseparable;
- returnable objects arise organically.

Deep question:

> **WHEN DOES REPAIR STOP BEING CARE AND BECOME ERASURE?**

---

## B. The Orchard

Why:
- continuity is visibly composite;
- cutting, grafting, succession, adaptation, weather, and inheritance are already inside the subject;
- material process can provide independent standing without human exploitation;
- return can be physical and seasonal.

Deep question:

> **WHAT DOES IT MEAN TO PRESERVE A LIVING THING THAT CAN SURVIVE ONLY BY CHANGING?**

---

## C. The Family Business Ledger

Why:
- stewardship has actual cost;
- records, contracts, employees, machines, and succession create multiple external jurisdictions;
- "saving" something can distribute cost invisibly;
- obligation can change without making prior stewardship false.

Deep question:

> **WHEN DOES SAVING AN INSTITUTION CEASE TO BE THE SAME AS CARING FOR THE PEOPLE AND RELATIONS THAT MADE IT WORTH SAVING?**

---

## D. The Unfinished Score

Why:
- custody of prior authorship is explicit;
- completion versus preservation creates a natural jurisdictional boundary;
- the same musical phrase can carry different standing;
- the handoff can come from independent archival material.

Deep question:

> **WHAT DOES A STEWARD OWE TO A WORK THAT CANNOT TELL THEM WHETHER IT WANTS TO BE FINISHED?**

---

# Important Non-Decision

No seed is selected as the story.

The seeds are evidence about what kinds of worlds can carry the architecture.

The current comparison suggests a deeper common structure:

> **THE KEEPER'S VIRTUE BECOMES THE SOURCE OF THE NEXT PROBLEM.**

The repairer repairs well.

The grower prunes well.

The operator saves well.

The steward completes responsibly.

Then contact reveals:

THE VERY PRACTICE
THAT MADE THEM
A GOOD KEEPER

MAY NO LONGER
BE SUFFICIENT.

This is stronger than:
THE KEEPER WAS WRONG.

It preserves first-pass dignity.

2. Can the handoff come from genuine independent standing?
3. Can the same three anchors survive both readings?
4. Can the changed relation alter obligation rather than merely knowledge?
5. Can the second reading remain non-final?
6. Can the core survive without the live seam?
7. Can the seed avoid becoming an obvious allegory of The Outside?
8. Can the receiver care before understanding the architecture?
9. Can different receivers notice different deltas?
10. Does the world remain alive after the central encounter?

Do not choose a winner until this stress test is complete.
