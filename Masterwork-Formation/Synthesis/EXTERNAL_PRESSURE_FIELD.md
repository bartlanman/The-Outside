# External Pressure Field — Before Inciting Incident

## Status

Pre-plot pressure research.

This document does **not** establish:
- the family Other as parent or child;
- the triggering event;
- plot sequence;
- who moves;
- who leaves;
- who is ill;
- marriage outcome;
- a deadline date.

It asks:

> **WHAT EXTERNAL CONDITION MAKES WAITING IMPOSSIBLE WITHOUT FEELING LIKE A PLOT DEVICE?**

Current need architecture:

### A
**PAST MUST RETAIN STANDING.**

### B
**PRESENT MUST ACQUIRE STANDING.**

### FAMILY OTHER
**ACTION MUST OCCUR IN THE WORLD.**

Current motion:

**HISTORY
⇄
EMERGENCE
⇄
CONSEQUENCE**

---

# I. Core Requirement

The external pressure should not manufacture the conflict.

It should reveal that the current arrangement can no longer hold the needs already present.

Working law:

> **GOOD EXTERNAL PRESSURE DOES NOT CREATE THE PROBLEM. IT REMOVES THE POSSIBILITY OF POSTPONING IT.**

The pressure should therefore be:

- ordinary enough to belong to real life;
- consequential enough to require action;
- distributed enough to affect all three people;
- materially specific;
- not morally pre-decided;
- capable of producing scenes beyond argument.

---

# II. Candidate Pressure 1 — Household Reconfiguration

## General form

The existing household arrangement can no longer remain exactly as it is.

Possibilities include:
- someone leaving;
- someone returning;
- someone moving in;
- space being repurposed;
- support being redistributed;
- an old room no longer serving its old function.

Do not choose the exact form yet.

## Why it works

Households materialize relationship assumptions.

A household contains:
- rooms;
- routines;
- money;
- privacy;
- ownership;
- care;
- habit;
- stored objects;
- bodily patterns.

Changing the household forces abstract identity into material decision.

## A pressure

A sees:
- accumulated decisions;
- prior commitments;
- what the space was built to support;
- cost of changing it.

## B pressure

B sees:
- current mismatch;
- unused capacity;
- possibility;
- need to let space reflect present life.

## Family Other pressure

The third person has a real need for:
- space;
- independence;
- care;
- departure;
- return;
- changed boundary.

## Literary strength

Exceptional.

It naturally produces:
- objects;
- rooms;
- packing;
- cleaning;
- repair;
- humor;
- territoriality;
- memory;
- practical decisions.

## Risk

Can become too symbolically neat if "the house = the marriage."

Protect by keeping the house inconvenient, ordinary, material.

## Status

**LEAD CANDIDATE.**

---

# III. Candidate Pressure 2 — Care Reconfiguration

## General form

The current care arrangement is no longer sufficient.

The family Other needs:
- more care;
- less care;
- different care;
- transferred responsibility;
- greater independence.

## Why it works

Care immediately creates:
- time;
- labor;
- scheduling;
- money;
- bodily consequence;
- obligation.

It also tests the earlier discovery:

> **LOVE MAY INCLUDE RECOGNITION WITHOUT BEING REDUCIBLE TO RECOGNITION.**

## A pressure

A may emphasize:
- continuity;
- obligation;
- prior promises;
- what has already been carried.

## B pressure

B may emphasize:
- present condition;
- changed capacity;
- what no longer works;
- need to redesign care.

## Family Other pressure

The third person's body or independence has standing of its own.

## Literary strength

Very high.

## Risk

Caregiving can overtake the marriage and moralize obligation.

A severe diagnosis would also create too much predetermined sympathy.

## Status

**VERY STRONG, ESPECIALLY IF ORDINARY RATHER THAN CATASTROPHIC.**

# IV. Candidate Pressure 3 — Financial Decision

## General form

A significant but non-catastrophic financial decision cannot be deferred.

Examples:
- education support;
- housing;
- repair / replacement;
- retirement allocation;
- care expense;
- job change.

## Why it works

Money converts:
- desire;
- promise;
- possibility;
- obligation;

into measurable consequence.

## A pressure

A sees:
- downstream cost;
- prior plans;
- commitments;
- opportunity cost.

## B pressure

B sees:
- sunk structure;
- present need;
- chance to redirect resources;
- cost of maintaining an obsolete plan.

## Family Other pressure

The third person may depend materially on the decision.

## Literary strength

High.

## Risk

Money alone may narrow the emotional field unless tied to another transition.

## Status

**STRONG SUPPORTING PRESSURE, NOT BEST PRIMARY FIELD.**

# V. Candidate Pressure 4 — Institutional Deadline

## General form

An external institution requires a decision by a date.

Possible domains:
- school;
- housing;
- medical care;
- insurance;
- employment;
- legal / administrative process.

## Why it works

The institution has jurisdiction independent of the family's readiness.

It creates a true outside clock.

## A pressure

A may respect:
- process;
- deadline;
- documented requirement.

## B pressure

B may resist:
- allowing institutional structure to dictate relational meaning.

## Family Other pressure

The third person cannot wait indefinitely.

## Literary strength

Moderate to high.

## Risk

Feels plot-mechanical if the deadline exists only to force action.

## Status

**BEST AS SECONDARY CLOCK ATTACHED TO A REAL HUMAN TRANSITION.**

---

# VI. Candidate Pressure 5 — Departure / Return

## General form

Someone is leaving or returning.

Could be:
- adult child;
- parent;
- one partner for work;
- family member after absence.

## Why it works

Departure and return automatically alter:
- space;
- routine;
- intimacy;
- dependence;
- memory.

The same person may return changed.

## A pressure

A sees:
- continuity;
- old role;
- promises;
- previous departures.

## B pressure

B sees:
- present person;
- new possibilities;
- need to revise expectations.

## Family Other pressure

The person leaving or returning has their own project.

## Literary strength

Very high.

## Risk

The event begins to resemble an inciting incident rather than a pressure class.

## Status

**STRONG, BUT HOLD ONE LEVEL ABOVE SPECIFIC EVENT.**

# VII. Candidate Pressure 6 — Body Change

## General form

A body no longer behaves according to the old arrangement.

This can be:
- age;
- pain;
- recovery;
- hormonal change;
- reduced stamina;
- increased capacity;
- changed sexuality.

## Why it works

The body is independent standing.

It does not wait for conceptual agreement.

## A pressure

A may interpret change through history and recurrence.

## B pressure

B may insist on present-state difference.

## Family Other pressure

Care or independence may be affected.

## Literary strength

Very high.

## Risk

Can become a medicalized plot if too dramatic.

## Status

**EXCELLENT EMBEDDED PRESSURE, NOT YET BEST PRIMARY FIELD.**

# VIII. Candidate Pressure 7 — Role Transition

## General form

A socially recognized role is ending or changing.

Examples:
- parenting role;
- caregiving role;
- work role;
- household role;
- family authority role.

## Why it works

Roles often outlive the conditions that created them.

A person may continue being treated as:
- child;
- caretaker;
- provider;
- organizer;
- dependent;

after reality has shifted.

## A pressure

A sees:
- continuity of responsibility;
- commitments attached to role.

## B pressure

B sees:
- role becoming obsolete;
- present identity exceeding it.

## Family Other pressure

The third person may be the one whose changed role forces revision.

## Literary strength

Exceptional.

## Risk

Too abstract unless embodied through space, money, body, or schedule.

## Status

**DEEP PRESSURE CLASS; SHOULD COMBINE WITH MATERIAL RECONFIGURATION.**

# X. Lead External Pressure

The strongest current pressure is:

> **A MATERIAL RECONFIGURATION OF THE SHARED HOUSEHOLD CAUSED BY A REAL FAMILY TRANSITION.**

This deliberately remains one level above event.

It can contain:
- aging parent;
- adult child;
- departure;
- return;
- altered care;
- changed financial support;
- room / privacy / access changes.

Its strength is that it translates all three time scales into material decisions.

A asks:
what does our history require us to preserve?

B asks:
what must change now?

Family Other asks:
what do I actually need from this household / family now?

---

# XI. Why Household Reconfiguration Is Stronger Than a Deadline

A deadline says:

DECIDE BY FRIDAY.

A household reconfiguration says:

THE OLD ARRANGEMENT NO LONGER FITS.

The deadline may later exist.

But the deeper pressure is spatial and relational.

Someone's:
- room;
- key;
- bed;
- transportation;
- money;
- privacy;
- belongings;
- place at the table;

must change.

That creates scene before explanation.

This obeys:

> **PUT THE ARGUMENT INTO THE OBJECT BEFORE PUTTING IT INTO THE DIALOGUE.**

---

# XII. The House Is Not the Marriage

Important protection:

Do not turn the house into a controlling metaphor.

The household matters because people actually:
- sleep there;
- store things there;
- pay for it;
- enter and leave;
- care for bodies there;
- negotiate privacy there.

If symbolic meaning emerges, it should emerge after material life.

Working rule:

> **SPACE MUST FUNCTION BEFORE IT SYMBOLIZES.**

---

# XIII. External Pressure and the Three Needs

The lead pressure activates each need differently.

## A

The material arrangement contains evidence of:
- shared planning;
- sacrifice;
- previous roles;
- commitments.

Changing it can feel like changing the standing of the past.

## B

The material arrangement may preserve:
- obsolete roles;
- unused routines;
- old definitions.

Changing it can make present life actionable.

## Family Other

The material arrangement may simply no longer serve what they need.

Their request or condition may have nothing to do with the marriage.

That is crucial.

---

# XIV. Strongest New Discovery — External Pressure Is a Capacity Test

The pressure does not merely force choice.

It tests whether the existing relational form can hold more reality.

The old arrangement once had enough capacity.

Now:
- A has changed;
- B has changed;
- the family Other has changed;
- the same arrangement is being asked to carry a larger or different field.

Therefore:

> **PRESSURE APPEARS WHEN REALITY EXCEEDS THE CAPACITY OF THE CURRENT RELATIONAL FORM.**

This connects directly with the project's Developmental Capacity work without requiring characters to know it.

---

# XV. The Ordinary Equivalent of a Threshold

The masterwork architecture has often used:
threshold,
crossing,
handoff.

At literary scale, the threshold need not be spectacular.

It may be:

- clearing a room;
- giving someone a key;
- taking a key back;
- signing a lease;
- choosing where a bed goes;
- deciding who drives whom;
- moving boxes;
- redirecting money;
- changing who is expected at dinner.

This may be the artistic correction:

> **A THRESHOLD CAN BE DOMESTIC BEFORE IT IS MYTHIC.**

The act matters because relation changes standing through it.

---

# XVI. Why the Pressure Should Not Be Catastrophic

A catastrophe would:
- simplify priorities;
- force sympathy;
- make action obvious;
- suppress ordinary ambiguity.

A non-catastrophic pressure is stronger because people still possess choices.

The family must decide what kind of relation it wants to become.

Therefore:

> **THE PRESSURE SHOULD BE SERIOUS ENOUGH TO REQUIRE ACTION, BUT OPEN ENOUGH THAT CHARACTER STILL MATTERS.**

---

# XVII. Contemporary Milieu Entry

The household reconfiguration also offers subtle entry for responsive systems.

People may use models to:
- compare options;
- draft difficult messages;
- make budgets;
- organize care;
- summarize conversations;
- think privately;
- generate alternative arrangements.

This can make each person's position more legible.

But the actual household still requires:
- bodies;
- keys;
- rooms;
- money;
- presence;
- refusal.

Therefore the model can assist representation while material reality keeps independent standing.

This is promising.

---

# XVIII. Anti-Plot Rules

Do not yet decide:
- who is moving;
- why;
- whether it is voluntary;
- which room;
- whose parent / child;
- the exact date;
- the exact financial problem;
- whether one partner proposes the change.

Do not use:
- eviction;
- terminal illness;
- sudden disaster;
- shocking inheritance;
- secret purchase;

unless later earned.

---

# XIX. Lead Pressure Architecture

Current compression:

**LEGITIMATE NEEDS
→ OLD ARRANGEMENT NO LONGER FITS
→ MATERIAL RECONFIGURATION REQUIRED
→ ACTION BEFORE FULL AGREEMENT
→ CONSEQUENCE CHANGES RELATION**

Or:

> **THEY DO NOT YET AGREE ON WHAT THE FAMILY IS BECOMING, BUT THEY MUST BEGIN MOVING THE FURNITURE.**

This is not dialogue.

It is the current literary compression.

# XX. Current Edge

We are now one step from event.

Before selecting an inciting incident, test the lead pressure through **ordinary threshold scenes**.

Generate micro-scenes such as:
- clearing a room;
- deciding whether someone keeps a key;
- moving stored boxes;
- measuring a doorway;
- buying a bed;
- changing a recurring payment;
- reassigning a parking space;
- deciding whose name stays on an account;
- packing / unpacking a cabinet.

For each, test whether:
1. all three standings can become present;
2. no one must be villain;
3. the object carries pressure before dialogue explains it;
4. the scene is worth reading without theory.
