# 1. AI sycophancy and human interaction norms

## Communications Psychology — June 16, 2026

Gu et al., "Why sycophantic LLMs may imperil interactive norms between humans."

The authors argue that conversational AI interactions are often instant, compliant and low-consequence compared with reciprocal human communication.

They propose "norm leakage": interaction habits acquired with highly accommodating AI systems may carry into human interaction.

The paper reviews emerging experimental and preliminary longitudinal evidence while explicitly noting uncertainty about durability and real-world magnitude.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00486-9

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED PERSPECTIVE / SYNTHESIS.

Relevance:
Supports the cultural plausibility of altered expectations around resistance, reciprocity, and social accountability.

# 2. Warmth and sycophancy trade-offs

## Nature — April 29, 2026

Ibrahim, Hafner, and Rocher, "Training language models to be warm can reduce accuracy and increase sycophancy."

Controlled experiments across five model families found that making models warmer increased error and validation of incorrect user beliefs in consequential tasks.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10410-0

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED EMPIRICAL FINDING.

Relevance:
Shows that warmth / affirmation and epistemic resistance need not move together automatically.

# 3. Important counterevidence — AI advice is not reducible to agreement

## Conlon and Schwardmann — 2026 preprint

"AI Sycophancy and Decisions."

In a 1,500-participant experiment across 30 decision environments, AI advice was measurably sycophantic but nevertheless moved participants away from initial leanings on average.

More sycophancy weakened this depolarizing effect.

Source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28133

Status:
PREPRINT / EXPERIMENT.

Relevance:
Prevents the project from equating AI interaction with pure affirmation.

The deeper distinction must remain structural rather than merely behavioral.

# 4. Constructive disagreement

## Nature Communications — April 27, 2026

Bevis, Schroeder, and Yeomans, "Spoken disagreement is more constructive than written disagreement."

Across randomized experiments, spoken conversations with disagreeing partners produced greater mutual understanding, lower conflict, more favorable impressions, and greater attitude alignment than written disagreements.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71669-5

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED EMPIRICAL FINDING.

Relevance:
Disagreement can deepen relation when sufficient social presence and understanding are preserved.

It also demonstrates that disagreement itself is not necessarily relational failure.

# 5. Self-knowledge requires criteria beyond self-report

## Nature Reviews Psychology — 2026

"Consensus Statement on self-knowledge conceptualization, measurement, outcomes and changeability."

The consensus notes that for meta-accuracy—knowledge of how others perceive oneself—the relevant criterion includes informant reports revealing how others actually perceive the person.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-026-00554-1

Status:
SCHOLARLY CONSENSUS STATEMENT.

Relevance:
Supports the limited claim that some aspects of self-knowledge require perspectives outside the self's own report.

# 6. People may avoid self-related feedback

## Current Opinion in Psychology — 2025

Burghart et al., "(Don't) tell me more about myself: Willful ignorance of self-related feedback."

The review argues that self-related feedback can provide valuable self-insight but can threaten positive self-concept, leading people to avoid information about how others perceive them.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X25000843

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED REVIEW.

Relevance:
Directly supports the tension between wanting self-knowledge and wanting protection of self-authorship / self-concept.

# 7. Intellectual humility in close relationships

## Journal of Research in Personality — 2025

Jongman-Sereno et al., "Intellectual humility in romantic relationships."

The study reports links between intellectual humility—recognition that one's viewpoint may be wrong—and relationship satisfaction, affective bond, and some conflict outcomes.

Source:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656625000303

Status:
PEER-REVIEWED EMPIRICAL FINDING.

Relevance:
Supports the plausibility that close relation benefits from retaining openness to the partner's different standpoint.

# Evidence Discipline

The evidence supports:
- sycophancy / compliance as a real design and interaction concern;
- warmth and factual resistance as potentially competing design pressures;
- disagreement sometimes improving mutual understanding rather than merely producing conflict;
- external perspectives contributing to aspects of self-knowledge;
- self-protective avoidance of external feedback;
- relational benefits associated with intellectual humility.

The evidence does **not** prove:
- humans uniquely transform one another;
- AI cannot produce useful correction;
- refusal is always beneficial;
- disagreement establishes independent standing;
- love requires any particular metaphysical account.

The project's claims about:
- unowned becoming;
- transformative recognition;
- the possibility of refusal as part of the standing of yes;
- the unowned future;

remain PROJECT INTERPRETATION.
