# Minimum Character Grounding Candidates

## Status

Comparative stress test.

All candidates preserve the same underlying perceptual pair:

### A
**CONTINUITY / PATTERN / LANGUAGE / OBLIGATION**

### B
**PRESENT / PARTICULAR / ACTION / POSSIBILITY**

The goal is not to choose final biography.

It is to discover which lived configuration produces the most literary life with the least visible engineering.

---

# Candidate 1 — The Long Shared Household With an Aging Parent

## Grounding

- A and B are both around 50.
- Together roughly 22 years.
- No dependent children at home.
- One aging parent has become a recurring practical presence.
- They are financially stable but retirement, medical care, and family support now make choices consequential.

### A
Material competence:
- good at multi-step planning;
- handles paperwork, schedules, accounts, recurring obligations;
- is the person others call when something complicated has to remain coherent for months.

Outside social world:
- respected for reliability and long memory;
- capable of improvisation, but does not enjoy depending on it.

Cross-pressure:
- once made a major personal break that surprised everyone.

### B
Material competence:
- strong in live, practical environments;
- can read changing conditions quickly;
- good with people under pressure;
- can rescue a bad day without announcing how.

Outside social world:
- others experience B as capable, funny, and immediately present.

Cross-pressure:
- keeps one long-standing ritual with almost stubborn loyalty.

## Why it works

Aging-parent pressure makes:
- memory;
- care;
- obligation;
- altered identity;
- bodily decline;

ordinary rather than thematic.

The marriage is not the only relation carrying history.

## Literary opportunities

- medical appointments;
- family stories;
- old objects;
- driving;
- medication;
- financial decisions;
- sibling pressure;
- care that must happen even during marital conflict.

## Risk

Caregiving can dominate the book and turn the marriage into a secondary plot.

## Theory exposure

Low.

## Contemporary-model entry

Natural through:
- scheduling;
- medical information;
- message drafting;
- family-memory organization;
- private reflection.

## Verdict

**VERY STRONG.**

# Candidate 2 — One Adult Child / Near-Adult Child Still in Orbit

## Grounding

- A and B are late 40s to early 50s.
- Together approximately 20 years.
- One child is in the late teen / early adult threshold.
- The household is beginning to lose its old organizing purpose.
- Money is stable but education, housing, and retirement create real tradeoffs.

### A
Material competence:
- good at long projects;
- anticipates downstream consequences;
- remembers family commitments and developmental history.

Outside social world:
- people rely on A to make complicated things actually happen.

Cross-pressure:
- has a private appetite for novelty that rarely becomes public.

### B
Material competence:
- good at present-state judgment;
- notices shifts in mood, need, and readiness;
- trusts action more than family narrative.

Outside social world:
- people come to B when plans have broken and someone has to decide what works now.

Cross-pressure:
- is fiercely loyal to one family tradition and refuses to abandon it.

## Why it works

The child becomes a third standing person who can see both parents differently.

The parents may disagree not only about each other, but about who the child is becoming.

This creates a rich generational mirror without making the child a plot device.

## Literary opportunities

- college / work transition;
- an empty bedroom;
- old school objects;
- family jokes losing their audience;
- parenting stories disputed by the person who was raised inside them;
- new privacy boundaries;
- money;
- separation from household routines.

## Risk

The work could drift into "empty nest marriage crisis," a familiar category.

The child must possess genuine standing and not merely trigger parental self-discovery.

## Theory exposure

Low to medium.

## Contemporary-model entry

Very natural because younger family members may use mediated language and tools differently, without turning the story into generational tech conflict.

## Verdict

**EXCEPTIONALLY STRONG.**

# Candidate 3 — No Children / A Dense Shared Social and Work Life

## Grounding

- A and B are early 50s.
- Together 23 years.
- No children.
- Their marriage has accumulated unusual weight because friends, work, travel, community, and household were built around the pair rather than around parenting.
- They have a wide shared social network.

### A
Material competence:
- good at maintaining long relational networks;
- remembers promises, histories, social obligations;
- skilled at language and negotiation in a practical rather than literary way.

Outside social world:
- A is often the person who preserves continuity among friends or groups.

Cross-pressure:
- has a reckless hobby or domain in which A does not plan at all.

### B
Material competence:
- improvises well;
- reads rooms quickly;
- acts before committees finish deciding;
- good at physical or practical problem-solving.

Outside social world:
- newer people often meet B as energetic and open-ended.

Cross-pressure:
- B keeps artifacts, music, objects, or rituals from the relationship with surprising fidelity.

## Why it works

Without children, the relationship itself has fewer external scripts.

A change in one partner has immediate consequences for the shared social identity of the pair.

Public introduction, couple performance, old stories, and friend-group memory become especially fertile.

## Literary opportunities

- dinner parties;
- travel;
- friends divorcing;
- weddings / funerals;
- being "the couple everyone knows";
- public stories about the marriage;
- friends who knew one version of each person.

## Risk

Could become socially narrow or privileged unless work and material stakes are substantial.

## Theory exposure

Very low.

## Contemporary-model entry

Natural through private reflection, social communication, travel planning, language, and reputation.

## Verdict

**VERY STRONG, ESPECIALLY FOR SOCIAL COMEDY.**

# Candidate 4 — Shared Material Project Without Shared Profession

## Grounding

- A and B are around 50.
- Together 18–21 years.
- They do not work together professionally.
- They do share one long-horizon material commitment:
  - a house repeatedly renovated;
  - a property;
  - a community project;
  - another durable thing requiring years of decision and labor.
- The object is not symbolic at first. It is simply part of their life.

### A
Material competence:
- tracks sequence, cost, prior decisions, maintenance, and contracts;
- remembers why earlier choices were made.

Outside social world:
- respected in a role where long-horizon judgment matters.

Cross-pressure:
- once insisted on a radical change to the shared project.

### B
Material competence:
- sees when the old plan no longer fits;
- notices physical conditions;
- can solve things on site;
- cares about how the space or material actually feels now.

Outside social world:
- valued for quick judgment and practical adaptation.

Cross-pressure:
- refuses to replace one old element because its continuity matters personally.

## Why it works

The shared material world gives the relationship physical consequence.

Arguments can enter through:
- walls;
- tools;
- repairs;
- receipts;
- rooms;
- use patterns.

It strongly supports the law:

> **PUT THE ARGUMENT INTO THE OBJECT BEFORE PUTTING IT INTO THE DIALOGUE.**

## Literary opportunities

- repair;
- choosing whether to replace;
- rooms carrying old versions of life;
- a space designed for a use that no longer exists;
- stored objects;
- financial cost.

## Risk

The material project could become too obviously symbolic if the book leans on it.

It also edges toward the earlier stewardship architecture, which is useful but dangerous.

## Theory exposure

Medium.

## Contemporary-model entry

Natural but secondary.

## Verdict

**STRONG, BUT ARCHITECTURAL GRAVITY IS TOO VISIBLE.**

# Strongest Result

No candidate dominates cleanly.

That is useful.

Two configurations lead for different reasons:

## Candidate 1 — Aging Parent

Best for:
- care;
- body;
- obligation;
- historical memory;
- external standing;
- reversal of dependence.

Deep pressure:

> **A PERSON CAN BE CHANGING WHILE SOMEONE STILL NEEDS THEM TO REMEMBER WHO THEY HAVE ALWAYS BEEN.**

## Candidate 2 — Adult / Near-Adult Child

Best for:
- generational self-perception;
- humor;
- social world;
- third perspective;
- old family identity becoming unstable.

Deep pressure:

> **THE PEOPLE WHO LEARNED WHO THEY WERE INSIDE OUR MARRIAGE MAY NOW SEE OUR MARRIAGE DIFFERENTLY THAN WE DO.**

Candidate 3 remains especially strong if the work ultimately wants greater social comedy and less family-duty gravity.

Candidate 4 should not lead because it pulls too strongly back toward architecture-through-object.

---

# Preferred Current Direction

Do not yet select Candidate 1 or Candidate 2.

Instead preserve a **family-generation field**:

- the couple is in midlife;
- one generation above or below them has enough standing to disturb the marriage's self-account;
- care / transition makes the family relation active;
- the external person is not merely a witness but has their own needs and interpretations.

This yields a three-position field:

**PARTNER A
⇄
PARTNER B
⇄
FAMILY OTHER**

The third person can:
- confirm neither;
- misread both;
- remember differently;
- need something practical;
- make the couple's private self-description socially consequential.

This may be richer than selecting parent or child too early.

# New Discovery — The Marriage Needs an Outside Witness Who Is Not a Referee

The couple cannot be the only interpreters of the relationship.

But an external character should not exist to tell the reader who is right.

Better:

> **THE OUTSIDE PERSON SHOULD HAVE THEIR OWN LIFE THAT MAKES THE MARRIAGE MATTER TO THEM FOR REASONS OTHER THAN INTERPRETING IT.**

An aging parent may need care.

An adult child may need separation.

A sibling may need cooperation.

This gives external standing without creating a therapist / judge function.

Working law:

> **THE THIRD PERSON SHOULD BE A PARTICIPANT IN CONSEQUENCE, NOT A COMMENTATOR ON THE THEME.**

---

# New Discovery — Biography Begins Becoming Plot When Needs Interlock

We are approaching the point where biography can no longer remain purely descriptive.

The moment:
- A needs something;
- B needs something incompatible;
- a third person needs something from both;

the field begins generating narrative.

That means the next step must be careful.

Do not plot yet.

First define:

> **WHAT KINDS OF PRESENT-TENSE NEEDS CAN MAKE THESE THREE STANDINGS CONSEQUENT WITHOUT PREDETERMINING THE STORY?**

Next object:

**PRESENT-TENSE NEED FIELD**

Not:
inciting incident.

Not:
plot outline.

Just:
what each person currently needs from life, from the relationship, and from the shared world.
