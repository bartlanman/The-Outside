# Research Instrument — Version 11

Use this instrument for every Masterwork Formation dossier.

The order matters. Historical reconstruction should precede contemporary translation.

## 1. Identity / unit of analysis
- Author / source / tradition
- Work
- Composition, performance, textualization, publication dates where relevant
- Existing role in The Outside constellation
- Unit of analysis:
  - individual author?
  - single work?
  - cycle?
  - corpus?
  - tradition?
  - collaborative or evolving system?
  - canon?

Question:
WHAT EXACTLY ARE WE STUDYING?

## 2. Archive condition / evidentiary survivability
Describe what evidence of making actually survives.

Possible statuses:
- LOW
- PARTIAL
- RICH
- EXCEPTIONAL

Record:
- manuscripts
- notebooks
- drafts
- letters
- proofs
- interviews
- recordings
- performance evidence
- papyri
- scribal witnesses
- translations
- editorial records
- canon lists
- contemporary testimony
- lost or disputed evidence

Question:
WHAT CAN WE ACTUALLY KNOW ABOUT THE MAKING?

## 3. Inheritance
Separate where possible:
- intellectual
- literary / formal
- religious / philosophical
- cultural / political
- technical / material
- traditional / performative
- prior texts
- earlier works by the same maker or tradition

Then ask:
WHAT DID THE WORK DO TO WHAT IT RECEIVED?

Track:
- selection
- recombination
- transformation
- refusal
- recontextualization
- recomposition
- supplementation
- stabilization

Distinguish:
SOURCE
from
GENERATIVE TRADITION
from
PRIOR AUTHORITATIVE TEXT.

## 4. Pressure
Include only pressures relevant to formation.

Possible kinds:
- historical
- political
- personal
- economic
- institutional
- technological
- linguistic
- bodily
- performative
- publication / patronage / censorship
- transmission
- communal / liturgical

For each major pressure ask:

PRESSURE
-> WHAT FORMAL PROBLEM?
-> WHAT FORMAL RESPONSE?

## 5. Problem trajectory

### Initial problem
What problem can be documented or reasonably inferred near the beginning?

### Developing problem
Did the problem change during formation?

### Operative problem
What problem does the finished or stabilized form make the receiver undergo?

### Traditional problem
If private intention is unrecoverable:
WHAT PROBLEM MUST THE TRADITION SOLVE TO CONTINUE EXISTING?

### Corpus problem
If the unit is multiple works:
WHAT PROBLEM MUST DISTINCT WRITINGS SOLVE TO BECOME A DURABLE RELATIONAL WHOLE?

### Migration
Did:
- essay become fiction?
- theology become journey?
- criticism become world?
- oral song become text?
- performance become script?
- local writing become corpus member?
- translation become new inheritance?
- one genre become another?

## 6. Constraints
- money
- time
- health
- medium
- memory
- meter
- performance
- length
- language
- audience
- publisher
- market
- censorship
- patronage
- technology
- available form
- transmission system
- inherited authority
- communal use

Ask:
WHICH CONSTRAINTS REMAINED LIMITS?
WHICH BECAME PRODUCTIVE FORM?

## 7. Formation dynamics
Describe how the work actually became the work.

Possible dimensions:
- blueprint <-> emergence
- expansion <-> compression
- sequential <-> fragmentary
- modular <-> continuous
- fixed <-> recomposable
- solitary <-> collaborative <-> traditional
- manuscript revision <-> performance recomposition <-> redaction
- stable ending <-> moving ending
- revision before publication <-> revision after publication
- local work <-> collection <-> canon
- fixed corpus <-> evolving corpus
- oral <-> textual <-> hybrid

Track:
- revision topology
- movement of fragments
- performance variation
- supplementation
- scale changes
- abandoned structures
- post-publication changes
- textualization
- translation
- editorial stabilization
- collection
- canon formation

## 8. Decisive formal / relational move
State the move as an operation, not a style label.

Question:
WHAT FORMAL, TRADITIONAL, OR RELATIONAL ACHIEVEMENT MADE THE PROBLEM EXPERIENCABLE?

For distributed traditions, the move may not be one recoverable individual's decision.

## 9. Rejected / transformed alternatives
Where evidence permits:
- rejected openings
- abandoned forms
- deleted structures
- changed endings
- displaced episodes
- alternate performances
- variant texts
- excluded writings
- editorial interventions
- alternate collections / orders

Classify:
- DELIBERATE REFUSAL
- PRACTICAL CONSTRAINT
- ACCIDENT
- PERFORMANCE VARIATION
- EDITORIAL INTERVENTION
- TRANSMISSION VARIANT
- CANONICAL EXCLUSION
- UNKNOWN

## 10. Embodiment / realization

If the work requires realization by another person, body, company, device, or interactive system, ask:
- Who completes the work experientially?
- What can that realizer change?
- What remains fixed?
- What becomes variable?
- Does casting or embodiment materially alter meaning?
- Is the work still the same work across different realizations?

Distinguish:
AUTHORSHIP
from
REALIZATION.

## 11. Voice / performance as function
Study where relevant:
- sentence architecture
- diction / register
- rhythm
- meter
- formula
- type-scene
- punctuation
- stance
- scale changes
- narrative distance
- performance presence

Always ask:
WHAT DOES THIS FEATURE DO?

## 12. Attention
What does this maker, editor, or tradition repeatedly notice?

Track recurring attentional habits.

Attention may be deeper than syntax as a source of voice.

## 13. Receiver operation
Use receiver rather than reader when the medium requires it.

Ask:
WHAT MUST THE RECEIVER DO FOR THE WORK TO COMPLETE ITS OPERATION?

Possibilities:
- traverse
- wait
- forget
- infer
- compare
- cross-read
- misread
- revise
- doubt
- recognize
- choose
- remember
- anticipate
- respond
- perceive variation
- supply inherited competence

## 14. Relational assembly / corpus effect
If the work enters a collection, cycle, canon, or durable field of other works, ask:

WHAT CHANGES BECAUSE OF THE RELATION?

Track:
- adjacency
- order
- intertextual reinterpretation
- changed standing
- mutual interpretability
- local identity versus corpus identity
- collection-level meaning

Question:
WHAT CAN THE CORPUS SAY THAT NO SINGLE LOCAL WORK COULD SAY ALONE?

## 15. Boundary operation
For collections and corpora ask:
- What counts as inside?
- What remains outside?
- Who or what sets the boundary?
- Was it disputed?
- Did different communities preserve different boundaries?
- Does the archive preserve the remainder?

Do not mistake a final boundary for historical inevitability.

## 16. Authorship locus / realization locus
Before assigning an office, map separately:

A. where creative / custodial authorship is located;
B. where experiential realization is located.

A performer, actor, reader, interface, or participant may realize a work without being a textual coauthor.

Possible loci:
- inherited tradition
- individual maker
- performer
- collaborator
- audience
- scribe
- redactor
- translator
- collector
- editor
- publisher
- institution
- community
- transmitter
- technological system

Ask:
WHO OR WHAT CAN CHANGE THE WORK?
WHO OR WHAT IS ANSWERABLE FOR THE VERSION OR RELATION THAT APPEARS?

## 17. Local standing / plurality test
If the work contains multiple voices, positions, characters, agents, or perspectives, ask:
- What gives each position local standing?
- Can it act or only speak?
- Can it resist the central system?
- Does it have durable commitments or memory?
- Are consequences real inside the work?
- Can the central author/system silently rewrite it?

Question:
IS THIS REAL PLURALITY, OR MULTIPLICITY GENERATED FROM ONE UNCONTESTED CENTER?

## 18. Permeability / identification test
If the speaker, author, narrator, system, or persona crosses into other identities or claims to include them, ask:
- What remains locally specific to the other?
- What cannot be absorbed?
- Can the other contradict the central voice?
- Does contact alter the central self?
- Is symbolic inclusion being mistaken for equality?
- Does empathy become appropriation?
- Does catalogue become inventory?
- Can the future receiver refuse the author's preferred self-understanding?

Question:
IS THE SELF BECOMING MORE RELATIONAL,
OR MERELY LARGER?

## 19. Editorial exposure / curation test
If editors, collaborators, curators, or external readers materially participate, ask:
- Who could say no?
- Which cuts or alterations were accepted?
- Did intervention change the work's identity?
- Is the contributor an editor, collaborator, coauthor, or something else?
- Does the final work conceal the intervention that produced it?

For inherited or allusive material ask:
- Who selected the source?
- What was excluded?
- Was the source preserved, transformed, translated, or tokenized?
- Does the selected corpus masquerade as "the tradition"?

## 20. Provenance / interpretation distinction
For quoted, cited, translated, inherited, or retrieved material ask:
- What is the provenance?
- What happens to it in the new context?
- What relation does placement create?
- What does the receiver have to reconstruct?

Question:
HAVE WE IDENTIFIED THE SOURCE,
OR EXPLAINED THE OPERATION?

## 21. Participation / scripted participation test
If the work or system makes the receiver an active participant, ask:
- What must the participant actually do?
- Can participation alter interpretation only, content, sequence, or the governing conditions?
- Is the participant choosing among pre-authored possibilities?
- Can the participant refuse the system's model of them?
- Is responsiveness being mistaken for agency?
- Is the participant necessary to the operation without being a coauthor?

Question:
CAN THE PARTICIPANT CHANGE THE CONDITIONS,
OR ONLY MOVE WITHIN THEM?

## 22. Generation / meaning distinction
If the work uses combinatorics, procedural systems, algorithmic generation, or large possibility spaces, ask:
- What does the system generate?
- Who or what selects?
- Where does consequence enter?
- What gives one possibility significance over another?
- Does meaning require an encounter beyond the generation mechanism?

Question:
HAVE WE PRODUCED POSSIBILITY,
OR EARNED MEANING?

## 23. Hermeticity / exit test
If the work builds a powerful internal system, institution, game, corpus, or world, ask:
- What can contradict it from outside?
- Does internal coherence substitute for external answerability?
- Can the system historicize itself?
- Can it recognize the difference between preserving value and preserving itself?
- What route of exit exists?
- Can something valuable be carried out without treating exit as total repudiation?
- Does particular responsibility interrupt universal abstraction?

Question:
IS THE SYSTEM STILL IN RELATION,
OR ONLY RELATING ITS OWN CONTENTS?

## 24. Mastery / relinquishment test
If the work contains mastery, expertise, initiation, or office, ask:
- What does mastery make possible?
- What does mastery conceal?
- Does the master become identified with the office?
- Can mastery reveal the system's insufficiency?
- Can the office be relinquished without nihilism?

Question:
IS MASTERY THE END STATE,
OR A THRESHOLD TO A DIFFERENT RESPONSIBILITY?

## 25. World-independence test
If the work constructs a world, environment, simulation, or experiential field, ask:
- What would still exist if the participant never arrived?
- What histories predate the protagonist?
- What agents pursue goals unrelated to the participant?
- What continues offstage?
- What places remain unvisited?
- What constraints push back against narrative convenience?
- Does the world change while the participant is elsewhere?

Question:
IS THIS A WORLD,
OR SCENERY ORCHESTRATED AROUND THE PARTICIPANT?

## 26. Return / non-restoration test
If the participant returns, ask:
- Is the traveler the same?
- Is home the same?
- What wounds or capacities remain?
- Does the return require local action?
- Does the return become stewardship, departure, grief, or renewed obligation?

Question:
DOES RETURN MEAN RESTORATION,
OR A NEW ENCOUNTER BETWEEN CHANGED TERMS?

## 27. Sub-creation / possession test
For world-making ask:
- What remains beyond the maker's explanation?
- What constraints does the maker voluntarily submit to?
- Can the world resist protagonist convenience?
- Does care become domination?
- Is the world being stewarded or possessed?

Question:
DOES THE MAKER BUILD A WORLD,
OR A PRIVATE WILL WITH SCENERY?

## 28. Revision / erasure test
If a work, world, corpus, or system changes across time, ask:
- Is the earlier state still visible?
- What changed in the author / system?
- What changed in the world / corpus?
- What new position or evidence made revision possible?
- Does the later work change the standing of the earlier work without deleting it?
- Can the system state what it used to believe or assume?
- Does the version history remain part of the meaning?

Question:
CAN THE WORK LEARN
WITHOUT FALSIFYING ITS OWN PAST?

## 29. Deeper-history / retcon test
When later work changes an established rule or worldview, ask:
- Is the change explained through deeper history, genealogy, suppressed evidence, or new position?
- Was the earlier claim locally warranted even if incomplete?
- Does the new account preserve continuity?
- Is the change merely authorial preference dressed as discovery?

Question:
IS THIS RE-VISION,
OR SOVEREIGN OVERWRITE?

## 30. Author-as-inheritor test
For return to prior work ask:
- What does the later author inherit from the earlier author?
- Which prior commitments now constrain revision?
- What must be preserved as evidence?
- What can legitimately change?
- How do readers function as custodians of prior versions?

Question:
CAN AUTHOR_1 RECEIVE AUTHOR_0
AS AN INHERITANCE RATHER THAN A MISTAKE TO DELETE?

## 31. Failure mode
Ask:

WHAT DOES THE WORK'S SUCCESSFUL TECHNIQUE BECOME WHEN IT EXCEEDS ITS WARRANT?

For corpora also ask:
WHAT DOES RELATION BECOME WHEN IT ERASES DIFFERENCE OR REMAINDER?

## 32. Outside / independent standing
Do not force an Outside.

Ask:
- What could refuse the maker, editor, or tradition?
- What actually changed the work without being authored by it?
- What evidence, audience, person, institution, history, material condition, event, body, inherited form, competing version, or excluded work had independent standing?
- Did the work absorb the encounter, resist it, or change because of it?

## 33. Authorial office
Assign only after the preceding sections.

Possible offices may include:
- carrier
- carrier-composer
- custodian
- custodian of relation
- architect
- curator
- reviser
- grower
- counter-architect
- participant
- witness
- steward
- depositor

Do not force a fit.

## 34. Work-specific vs author/tradition-specific
Ask:
- What recurs across the maker's oeuvre?
- What recurs across the tradition?
- What belongs only to this work?
- What is a stable habit?
- What is a response to this pressure?
- What belongs only to collection or canon context?

## 35. Reception / continued formation
Reconstruct:
- expected receiver
- genre expectation
- performance conditions
- publication conditions
- communal / liturgical use
- contemporary response
- misunderstanding
- controversy
- early commentary
- institutional response

Then ask:

DID RECEPTION OCCUR AFTER FORMATION,
OR DID RECEPTION PARTICIPATE IN FORMATION OR STATUS CHANGE?

## 36. Deposit / succession
Ask:
- What was actually deposited?
- Was deposit singular or iterative?
- Was it local or relational?
- What did later receivers come upon?
- What survived authorial explanation?
- What survived the originating worldview?
- What later uses could not have been anticipated?
- What remained generative after historical conditions changed?

Distinguish:
WHAT A MAKER OR TRADITION ATTEMPTED TO CARRY
from
WHAT LATER RECEIVERS FOUND.

## 37. Surviving operation
Remove period furniture.

State:
- the deep operation
- what must survive
- what can disappear
- the operation's failure mode

## 38. THEN -> NOW
Only after historical reconstruction.

Ask:
- What contemporary pressure occupies an analogous structural position?
- What has changed so much that literal replication would fail?
- What formal or relational operation today might produce an analogous effect?

Distinguish:
SURFACE
-> TECHNIQUE
-> FUNCTION
-> HISTORICAL-EQUIVALENT CREATION.

## 39. Contemporary question
End with one earned question.

Do not end with a prescription unless the dossier has actually earned one.
