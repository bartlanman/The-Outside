# Research Instrument — Version 2

Use this instrument for every Masterwork Formation dossier.

The order matters. Historical reconstruction should precede contemporary translation.

## 1. Identity
- Author / source / tradition
- Work
- Composition and publication dates
- Existing role in The Outside constellation
- Unit of analysis: single work, cycle, corpus, tradition?

## 2. Archive condition / evidentiary survivability
Describe what evidence of making actually survives.

Possible descriptive statuses:
- LOW
- PARTIAL
- RICH
- EXCEPTIONAL

Record:
- manuscripts
- notebooks
- drafts
- letters
- proofs
- interviews
- editorial records
- contemporary testimony
- lost or disputed evidence

Question:
WHAT CAN WE ACTUALLY KNOW ABOUT THE MAKING?

Do not infer rejected alternatives when the archive cannot support them.

## 3. Inheritance
Separate:
- intellectual inheritance
- literary / formal inheritance
- religious / philosophical inheritance
- cultural / political inheritance
- technical / material inheritance
- earlier works by the same author

Then ask:
WHAT DID THE WORK DO TO WHAT IT RECEIVED?

Track:
- selection
- recombination
- transformation
- refusal
- recontextualization

## 4. Pressure
Include only pressures relevant to formation.

Possible kinds:
- historical
- political
- personal
- economic
- institutional
- technological
- linguistic
- bodily
- publication / patronage / censorship

For each important pressure ask:

PRESSURE
-> WHAT FORMAL PROBLEM?
-> WHAT FORMAL RESPONSE?

Do not let biography become causal merely because it is interesting.

## 5. Problem trajectory

### Initial problem
What problem can be documented or reasonably inferred near the beginning?

### Developing problem
Did the problem change while the work was being made?

### Operative problem
What problem does the finished form actually make the reader undergo?

### Migration
Did:
- essay become fiction?
- theology become journey?
- criticism become world?
- history become structure?
- one genre become another?

Do not assume the final problem existed fully at the beginning.

## 6. Constraints
- money
- time
- health
- medium
- length
- language
- audience
- publisher
- market
- censorship
- patronage
- technology
- available form

Ask:
WHICH CONSTRAINTS REMAINED LIMITS?
WHICH BECAME PRODUCTIVE FORM?

## 7. Formation dynamics

Describe how the work actually grew.

Possible dimensions:
- blueprint <-> emergence
- expansion <-> compression
- sequential <-> fragmentary
- modular <-> continuous
- stable ending <-> moving ending
- revision before publication <-> revision after publication
- solitary <-> collaborative
- fixed corpus <-> evolving corpus

Track:
- revision topology
- movement of fragments
- scale changes
- abandoned structures
- post-publication changes
- recomposition

Question:
HOW DID THE WORK BECOME THE WORK?

## 8. Decisive formal move
State the move as an operation, not a style label.

Examples:
- make the reader traverse the order
- make later time revise earlier perception
- borrow documentary authority and place fiction inside it

Question:
WHAT FORMAL DECISION MADE THE PROBLEM EXPERIENCABLE?

## 9. Rejected / transformed alternatives
Where evidence permits:
- rejected openings
- abandoned forms
- deleted structures
- changed endings
- material moved elsewhere
- editorial interventions
- alternatives preserved in drafts

Classify:
- DELIBERATE REFUSAL
- PRACTICAL CONSTRAINT
- ACCIDENT
- EDITORIAL INTERVENTION
- UNKNOWN

## 10. Voice as function
Study:
- sentence architecture
- diction / register
- rhythm
- punctuation
- stance
- scale changes
- narrative distance

But always ask:
WHAT DOES THIS FEATURE DO?

Do not stop at stylistic description.

## 11. Attention
What does this author notice that another author might pass over?

Track recurring attentional habits:
- position
- social rank
- temporal recurrence
- authority
- bodily sensation
- cost
- sign
- contradiction
- landscape
- language
- relation

Attention may be deeper than syntax as a source of voice.

## 12. Reader operation
This is distinct from tone or audience.

Ask:
WHAT MUST THE READER DO FOR THE WORK TO COMPLETE ITS OPERATION?

Possibilities:
- traverse
- wait
- forget
- infer
- compare
- misread
- revise
- doubt
- recognize
- choose
- supply relation

## 13. Failure mode
Ask:

WHAT DOES THE WORK'S SUCCESSFUL TECHNIQUE BECOME WHEN IT EXCEEDS ITS WARRANT?

Examples:
- architecture -> totalization
- retrospection -> destiny
- coherence -> self-certification

This is often the constitutional limit of the form.

## 14. Outside / independent standing
Do not force an Outside.

Ask:
- What could refuse the author?
- What actually changed the work without being authored by the work?
- What evidence, person, institution, history, material condition, event, body, or reception had independent standing?
- Did the work absorb the encounter, resist it, or change form because of it?

If no strong evidence exists, record that.

## 15. Authorial office
Assign only after the preceding sections.

Possible offices may include:
- carrier
- custodian
- architect
- curator
- reviser
- grower
- counter-architect
- participant
- witness
- steward
- depositor

Do not force a fit.

Office remains provisional until comparison across works.

## 16. Work-specific vs author-specific
Compare with the author's other work.

Ask:
- What recurs?
- What belongs only to this work?
- What is a stable habit?
- What is a response to this pressure?

## 17. Landing / reception
Reconstruct:
- expected audience
- genre expectation
- publication conditions
- contemporary reception
- contemporary misunderstanding
- controversy
- early commentary
- institutional response

Do not narrate later canonization as though it were obvious at publication.

## 18. Deposit / succession
Ask:
- What was actually deposited?
- What did later readers come upon?
- What survived the author's explanation?
- What survived the originating worldview?
- What later uses could not have been anticipated?
- What remained generative after historical circumstances changed?

Distinguish:
WHAT THE AUTHOR MEANT TO LEAVE
from
WHAT LATER READERS FOUND.

## 19. Surviving operation
Remove period furniture.

State:
- the deep operation
- what must survive
- what can disappear
- the operation's failure mode

## 20. THEN -> NOW
Only after historical reconstruction.

Ask:
- What contemporary pressure occupies an analogous structural position?
- What has changed so much that literal replication would fail?
- What formal operation today might produce an analogous effect?

Distinguish:
SURFACE
-> TECHNIQUE
-> FUNCTION
-> HISTORICAL-EQUIVALENT CREATION.

## 21. Contemporary question
End with one earned question.

Do not end with a prescription unless the dossier has actually earned one.
